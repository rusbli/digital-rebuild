How many times a week does your phone ring with someone wanting to book?

If you're a hairdresser, a driving instructor, a dog groomer or a physio, the answer might be dozens. Each call interrupts whatever you're doing. Some come in while you're with a client, so you miss them. Some people simply won't call at all; they'll book with whoever lets them do it online at 10pm.

Online booking can fix that. But it isn't right for every business, and the choice of tool matters more than people expect.

> **Quick answer:** Online booking suits businesses with set appointment types and lengths, such as salons, instructors and treatments. Most small businesses use a separate booking tool linked or embedded into their website, rather than building their own. Before choosing, check the fees, how it works on a phone, whether it takes deposits, how reminders work and whether you can easily leave. For jobs that need a quote first, a good enquiry form often works better.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Is online booking right for my business?
- Should it be built into my website or a separate tool?
- What should I check before choosing one?

## Is online booking right for you?

Here's the first question. Online booking works best when:

- **Appointments are fairly standard.** A haircut, a 60-minute lesson, a 30-minute treatment.
- **Prices are known in advance.**
- **Customers book often**, or rebook regularly.
- **Lots of bookings happen out of hours**, when you can't answer the phone.

It works less well when:

- **Every job needs a visit or quote first**, like building work or bespoke projects.
- **The time needed varies a lot** depending on what the customer wants.
- **You need to check suitability** before agreeing, for safety or practical reasons.

For those, a short enquiry form that asks the right questions is usually the better choice. My guide to [websites that get visitors but no enquiries](/blog/website-not-getting-enquiries/) covers how to make that form work.

## Built in or separate?

Here's the second question. There are four common approaches:

| Approach | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A separate booking tool, linked from your site | A "Book now" button takes customers to the tool's booking page | Quick to set up, works with any website |
| A booking tool embedded in your site | The tool's booking calendar appears on one of your pages | Keeps customers on your site, looks more joined-up |
| A website builder's built-in booking | Booking comes as part of platforms like Wix or Squarespace | People already using that platform |
| Custom-built booking | Built specifically for your business | Unusual needs that off-the-shelf tools can't handle |

For most small businesses, a well-chosen separate tool, linked or embedded, is the simplest and most reliable option. Custom-built booking systems are rarely worth the cost unless your needs are genuinely unusual.

## What to check before choosing a tool

Here's the third question. Booking tools vary a lot. Before signing up, check:

### Cost

- Is it a monthly subscription, a fee per booking, or both?
- Are there extra fees for taking card payments or deposits?
- What does it cost as you grow, or add staff?

Check the provider's current pricing page, as prices change.

### The customer's experience on a phone

Try booking an appointment yourself, on your phone, as if you were a customer. Is it quick? Is it clear? Does it ask for more information than it needs?

### Deposits and no-shows

If no-shows cost you money, check whether the tool can take deposits, card details or cancellation fees, and how refunds work.

### Reminders

Automatic text or email reminders can reduce missed appointments. Check whether they're included or cost extra.

### Your calendar

Does it sync with the calendar you already use, so you don't get double-booked?

### Your data, and leaving

- Can you export your customer list and booking history if you leave?
- Where is customer data stored, and does the provider explain how it handles it?

If you collect personal information through a booking tool, mention it in your privacy notice. My guide to [privacy policies for small business websites](/blog/privacy-policy-small-business-website/) explains what to include.

## The aha: online booking changes who books

When you only take bookings by phone, you get the customers who are happy to phone.

Plenty of people, particularly younger customers, would much rather tap a few buttons than make a call. Others want to book late at night. Online booking doesn't just save you time; it can reach customers you weren't reaching at all.

The flip side: you lose a chance to chat, upsell or check details. Many businesses keep the phone option for anyone who prefers it.

## Making it work with your website

- **Put a clear "Book now" button** near the top of every relevant page, not just the contact page.
- **Show prices and appointment lengths** on your service pages, so people know what they're booking.
- **Explain your cancellation policy** before they book.
- **Make sure the booking page matches your site**, so customers don't feel they've been sent somewhere strange.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing on price alone** and ending up with a clunky mobile experience.
- **Hiding the booking button** on one page.
- **Offering every possible time**, then cancelling. Set your real availability.
- **Not testing it** as a customer.
