More and more people don't type "plumber Penarth" into Google any more. They ask.

"Who's a good plumber near Penarth for a boiler repair?" "What should a small business website cost?" "Find me a dog groomer in Barry that's open on Saturdays."

They ask ChatGPT, or Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, or the assistant on their phone. And instead of ten links, they get an answer, sometimes naming businesses.

So how do you become one of the names?

The honest answer is less mysterious than people selling "AI SEO" make it sound.

> **Quick answer:** AI search tools mostly draw on the same things search engines do: clear web pages, business listings and what others say about you. Google says there are no special requirements for its AI features beyond normal SEO best practice. So make sure your website clearly says what you do, where and for whom, answer real customer questions on your pages, keep your Google Business Profile complete, and build genuine reviews and mentions.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What do AI tools actually base their answers on?
- What does Google say about its AI features?
- What can a small business practically do?

## What AI answers are built from

Here's the first question. AI search tools don't magically know which businesses are good. They draw on information that's available to them, which often includes:

- **Web pages**, including your website and pages about you.
- **Business listings**, like map and directory listings.
- **Reviews and mentions** on other sites.
- **Their own search results**, for tools that search the web live.

Different tools work in different ways, and they change often. But the pattern is consistent: they repeat what's clearly and consistently said about you online.

If nothing clear is said, they have nothing to work with.

## What Google says about AI Overviews

Here's the second question. Google has published guidance on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). In short:

- Normal SEO best practices still apply.
- There are no extra special requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.
- Pages need to be indexed and eligible to appear in search with a snippet.
- Traffic from AI features is included in your overall search figures in Search Console.

So the foundation is the same as ordinary search. My [Google Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-for-small-business/) covers checking that your pages are indexed.

## What you can practically do

Here's the third question.

### 1. Say clearly what you do, where and for whom

AI tools can only repeat what they can find. Your website should say, in plain sentences:

- "Smith Heating is a Gas Safe registered heating engineer based in Barry, covering the Vale of Glamorgan and Cardiff."
- What services you offer.
- Who you help.

Vague slogans give a tool nothing to quote.

### 2. Answer real questions on your pages

People ask AI tools questions. Pages that answer those questions directly are more useful to them.

Think about what customers ask you on the phone: "How long does a rewire take?", "Do I need planning permission for a garden room?", "What's included in an end of tenancy clean?" Answer them clearly on your service pages and in short FAQs.

A good habit: start each answer with a direct, self-contained sentence, then add detail.

### 3. Keep your Google Business Profile complete

For local questions, business listings matter. A complete, accurate profile, with categories, services, hours and genuine reviews, gives tools and people the same clear picture. My guide to [showing up in near-me searches](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-near-me-searches/) covers the essentials.

### 4. Be consistent everywhere

Same business name, phone number, area and description across your website, profile and directory listings. Contradictory details make any tool less confident about you.

### 5. Earn genuine mentions and reviews

Reviews, local news, your trade body's directory, suppliers and community sites all help build a picture of a real, well-regarded business.

### 6. Write titles and descriptions that explain the page

Clear page titles help both search engines and AI tools understand what each page is about. My guide to [writing page titles and descriptions](/blog/page-titles-and-meta-descriptions/) explains how.

## The aha: AI rewards the businesses that explain themselves

Many small business websites never actually say what the business does in a plain sentence. They rely on photos, logos and slogans.

That's always been a weakness for search. With AI answers, it's a bigger one. A tool trying to answer "Who fits kitchens in Penarth?" can't use a page that only says "Quality craftsmanship since 2010".

The businesses that explain themselves clearly, on their own site and elsewhere, are the ones these tools can find and repeat.

## What to avoid

- **Paying for guaranteed AI recommendations.** Nobody can guarantee what these tools say.
- **Hidden text or tricks** aimed at AI tools. Google's spam policies still apply.
- **Mass-produced, low-value pages** written only to be picked up. They help nobody.
- **Inconsistent details** across the web.

## A simple plan for this month

1. Add a plain sentence to your home page saying what you do, where and for whom.
2. Add a short FAQ to each main service page, answering real customer questions.
3. Complete your Google Business Profile.
4. Check your business details match everywhere.
5. Ask five happy customers for reviews.

None of this is new. It's the same work that helps you in ordinary search, which is good news: you only have to do it once.
