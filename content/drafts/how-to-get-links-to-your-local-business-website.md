You've probably had the emails. "We can get you 500 high-quality backlinks for £49."

Links from other websites to yours do matter. They help people find you, and they're part of how search engines judge how well known a business is.

But the cheap shortcut is exactly what Google's rules warn against. The good news is that local businesses have plenty of honest ways to earn links, many of which they're already halfway to.

> **Quick answer:** For a local business, the best links come from real relationships: suppliers and manufacturers listing you as a stockist or installer, your trade body's member directory, local news, community groups and events you support, local business networks and genuine partners. Make sure each link uses your correct name and points to the right page. Avoid buying links or link schemes, which Google's spam policies prohibit.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Why do links matter for a local business?
- Where can I honestly get them?
- What should I avoid?

## Why links matter

Here's the first question. Links do two jobs.

**They bring people.** A link from your supplier's "find an installer" page or a local events page sends real visitors who are already interested.

**They help you get found.** Google's help page on [local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says prominence, how well known a business is, draws on information from across the web, including links.

Relevant local links tend to be worth far more than random ones from unrelated sites.

## Where to get links honestly

Here's the second question.

### Suppliers and manufacturers

If you're an approved installer, stockist or partner, many manufacturers have "find an installer" or "where to buy" pages. Ask to be listed, with a link to your website.

### Trade bodies and schemes

If you're a member of a trade body or registered with a scheme, make sure your directory listing is accurate and includes your website.

### Local news

A genuine local story can bring coverage and a link. My guide to [getting local press coverage](/blog/local-press-coverage-for-small-business/) explains how to find and pitch a story.

### Community involvement

Sponsoring a local team, supporting a charity event or helping a community building often comes with a mention on their website. Do it because it's worthwhile; the link is a bonus.

### Local business networks

Chambers of commerce and local business groups often have member directories.

### Genuine partners

Businesses you regularly work with, like a kitchen supplier and the fitter they recommend, can link to each other where it genuinely helps customers.

### Directories

A few relevant, accurate directory listings help too. My guide to [business directories](/blog/local-business-directories-uk/) explains which are worth claiming.

## What to avoid

Here's the third question. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe link spam as creating links mainly to manipulate rankings. Examples include:

- **Buying or selling links** for ranking purposes.
- **Excessive link swaps** ("link to me and I'll link to you").
- **Automated programs** that create links.
- **Low-quality directory or bookmark sites** created mainly for links.

Bulk link packages almost always fall into these categories. At best they waste your money; at worst they harm your visibility.

## The aha: you're probably owed links already

Many local businesses are already mentioned online without a link: in a supplier's list, a local news story, an event page, a community newsletter.

Search Google for your business name in quotes. Look for pages that mention you but don't link to your website. A polite email asking them to add a link, or correct your details, often works, because they already know you.

## Make links worth having

- **Check the details.** Correct business name, phone number and website address.
- **Point to the right page.** An installer listing should link to your relevant service page, not just your home page, where possible.
- **Have something worth linking to.** Useful service pages and guides give people a reason to link.

Before chasing links, make sure the basics are done, too. My guide on [checking your web designer did the SEO basics](/blog/has-my-web-designer-done-the-seo/) has ten checks.

## A simple plan

1. List every supplier, trade body and network you're connected to.
2. Check each for a directory or "find an installer" page, and ask to be listed with a link.
3. Search for unlinked mentions of your business and ask for links.
4. Look for one community project or local story this year.
5. Ignore anyone selling bulk links.
