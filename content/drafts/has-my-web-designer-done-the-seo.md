"Don't worry, it's all SEO optimised."

Lots of small business owners hear this when their website goes live. Few know how to check whether it's true.

You don't need to understand the technical side to spot whether the basics have been done. Here are ten checks you can do yourself, mostly in a browser, in about half an hour.

> **Quick answer:** Check that each page has a unique, descriptive title; each page has one clear main heading; your site appears in a site: search; there's a sitemap; the site loads on HTTPS; it works well on a phone; it loads reasonably quickly; the words say what you do and where; images have descriptions; and Google Search Console is set up with you as an owner.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What should be done as standard?
- How can I check without technical knowledge?
- What do I do if the basics are missing?

## What "the basics" means

Here's the first question. There's a difference between **SEO basics** and **ongoing SEO**.

The basics are part of building a website properly. They make sure Google can find, read and understand your pages. Any website build should include them.

Ongoing SEO is the longer job of improving your visibility for competitive searches over time: writing new content, earning mentions, refining pages. That's a separate piece of work.

These ten checks are about the basics.

## Ten checks you can do yourself

Here's the second question.

### 1. Page titles

Open each main page and look at the browser tab. Does it describe the page, like "Boiler servicing in Penarth | Smith Heating"? Or does every page say "Home" or just your business name?

My guide to [writing page titles and descriptions](/blog/page-titles-and-meta-descriptions/) shows what good ones look like.

### 2. One clear main heading

Each page should have one main heading that says what the page is about. Look at the biggest text at the top of each page. Is it descriptive, or a slogan?

### 3. Is Google indexing it?

Search Google for `site:yourbusiness.co.uk`. Your pages should appear. If nothing appears, something may be blocking Google.

### 4. A sitemap

Visit `yourbusiness.co.uk/sitemap.xml`. A list of your pages (or a list of sitemap files) should appear. If you get an error, there may not be one.

### 5. HTTPS

Does the address start with `https://`, with a padlock? If you see "Not secure", my guide to [fixing the 'Not secure' warning](/blog/website-says-not-secure/) explains what to do.

### 6. Mobile

Open the site on your phone. Is it easy to read and use without zooming?

### 7. Speed

Run your home page through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and look at the mobile results.

### 8. Clear wording

Read your home page and main service pages. Do they say plainly what you do and where? Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) stresses writing content that's helpful and uses the words people would search for.

### 9. Image descriptions

Images should have short text descriptions (alt text). Ask your designer, or check in your website's editor.

### 10. Google Search Console

Is it set up, and are you an owner? My [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-for-small-business/) explains how to check.

## Score your site

| Checks passed | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 9 or 10 | The basics are in good shape |
| 6 to 8 | A few gaps, usually quick to fix |
| 5 or fewer | The basics weren't done properly |

## What to do if the basics are missing

Here's the third question.

1. **Ask your designer.** Some fixes, like titles and Search Console, are quick. A good designer will happily put them right.
2. **Fix what you can yourself**, if you have access to your website's settings.
3. **Get a second opinion** if you're unsure, or if your designer isn't responding.

Be wary of anyone who:

- Promises guaranteed first-page rankings.
- Can't explain what they did in plain English.
- Charges a large monthly fee without telling you what's being done.

## The aha: the words matter more than the settings

It's easy to focus on technical settings. They matter, but they're the foundation, not the building.

The biggest SEO problem on most small business websites isn't a missing setting. It's that the pages never clearly say what the business does, where, and for whom. A perfectly configured page that says "Quality you can trust" still gives Google nothing to match.

So after the ten checks, read your pages as a stranger would. If you can't tell what the business does and where within a few seconds, that's the first thing to fix.

## A quick routine

1. Run the ten checks once now.
2. Fix the gaps, or ask your designer to.
3. Check Search Console monthly.
4. Recheck titles and headings whenever you add a new page.
