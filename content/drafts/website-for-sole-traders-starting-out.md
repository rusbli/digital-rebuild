You've done it. You've gone self-employed. You've told HMRC, got the van sorted and told everyone you know.

Now you need customers who **don't** know you. And for that, you need to exist online.

It's easy to overspend here, or to spend weeks on a website before you've had your first job. Here's what to set up first, in what order, and what can wait.

> **Quick answer:** Start with the free and cheap foundations: register your own domain name, set up a business email on it, and create a Google Business Profile. Then build a simple website, three to five pages, saying what you do, where, and how to contact you. Add photos and reviews as you get them. Don't spend heavily until you know what customers ask for.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What should I do first?
- What should my first website include?
- What can wait?

## The order that makes sense

Here's the first question.

### 1. Register your domain

Your domain name (yourname.co.uk or yourbusiness.co.uk) is cheap, and it's yours. Register it now, in your own name, even before you have a website. My guide to [choosing a domain name](/blog/choosing-a-domain-name-uk/) explains how.

### 2. Set up business email

hello@yourbusiness.co.uk looks far more established on a quote than a free webmail address. My guide to [business email on your own domain](/blog/business-email-with-your-own-domain/) covers the options.

### 3. Create a Google Business Profile

Free, and for many local trades the biggest source of calls. My [Google Business Profile guide for tradespeople](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/) covers setting it up, including keeping your home address private.

### 4. Build a simple website

Once those are in place, a small website ties it all together.

## Your first website

Here's the second question. Keep it simple:

- **Home**: what you do, where, and how to contact you, at the top.
- **Services**: one page, or a page each for your main services.
- **About**: who you are, with a photo. As a sole trader, you're the business, and customers like knowing who'll turn up.
- **Contact**: phone, email, a short form, your area.

Three to five pages is plenty. My guide on [one-page websites versus several pages](/blog/one-page-website-or-multi-page/) explains when to add more.

### When you don't have much to show yet

New businesses worry they've got nothing to put on their website. Use what you do have:

- **Your experience**, honestly described, even if it was working for someone else.
- **Your qualifications** and registrations.
- **Photos of your work** as soon as you have some. My guide to [taking photos with your phone](/blog/photos-for-your-website-with-a-phone/) helps.
- **How you work**: how you quote, how you communicate, how you leave the job.

Don't fill gaps with stock photos or made-up reviews. Honest and new beats polished and fake.

## What can wait

Here's the third question. You probably don't need these on day one:

- **Online booking**, unless your work is appointment-based.
- **A blog**, until you have time to write genuinely useful posts.
- **Paid advertising**, until your profile and website are working.
- **Lots of pages** for every possible service and town.
- **Expensive branding**, until you know your business well.

## Free versus paid

If money is very tight, a free website builder can get you started. But register your own domain anyway, so your address never has to change. My guide to [free versus paid websites](/blog/free-website-vs-paid-website/) explains the trade-offs.

## The aha: your first customers write your website

You don't need to guess what to put on your website. Your first customers will tell you.

Pay attention to the questions they ask on the phone, the words they use, and the worries they mention. "Will you take the old one away?" "Do you do evenings?" "Is there a call-out charge?"

Those questions are your website content. Add the answers to your pages, and your website gets more useful with every job.

## A starter checklist

1. Domain registered in your name?
2. Business email set up on it?
3. Google Business Profile created and verified?
4. Simple website: what, where, contact, about?
5. Asking every customer for a review?
6. Writing down the questions customers ask?
