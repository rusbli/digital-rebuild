A customer turns up on a Sunday and finds you closed. Google said you were open.

Or someone rings the number on your listing and gets a stranger, because it's the number you had three years ago.

Or worst of all, your listing says "Permanently closed", and you're very much still trading.

Wrong information on Google costs you customers quietly, without you ever knowing. Here's where it comes from and how to fix it.

> **Quick answer:** Start with your Google Business Profile: claim it if you haven't, then check and correct every detail, including hours, phone number, address, website and status. Then make sure your website and other listings show exactly the same information. Check your profile regularly for suggested edits, and update special hours for bank holidays.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Where does wrong information come from?
- How do I fix it?
- How do I stop it coming back?

## Where wrong information comes from

Here's the first question. Google builds its picture of your business from several sources:

- **Your Business Profile**, if you've claimed it.
- **Suggested edits** from members of the public.
- **Your website.**
- **Other listings and directories** around the web.

If those sources disagree, or your profile is out of date, Google can show the wrong details. Old directory listings with a previous phone number are a common cause.

## Step 1: claim your profile

If you haven't claimed your Business Profile, do that first. Until you manage it, you can only suggest edits like anyone else.

Search for your business on Google Maps and look for the option to claim or manage it. You'll need to verify. My guide to [Google Business Profile verification](/blog/google-business-profile-verification/) explains how.

If someone else (a former employee, an old designer) manages it, you can request access through Google.

## Step 2: correct every detail

Here's the second question. Go through your profile carefully:

- **Business name**: exactly as on your signs and paperwork.
- **Category**: what your business is.
- **Phone number.**
- **Website.**
- **Address or service area.**
- **Opening hours**, including special hours for bank holidays.
- **Status**: make sure it isn't marked as temporarily or permanently closed.
- **Services, description and photos.**

## Step 3: fix your website and other listings

Your website should show exactly the same details. Then search Google for your business name and old phone number, and correct any listings showing old information. My guide to [business directories](/blog/local-business-directories-uk/) explains which ones matter.

## Step 4: check for suggested edits

Members of the public can suggest edits to listings on Google Maps. Sometimes they're helpful; sometimes they're wrong. When you manage your profile, check it regularly so you can spot and correct anything that's changed.

## How to stop it coming back

Here's the third question.

1. **Check your profile monthly.** Five minutes.
2. **Set special hours** before every bank holiday and holiday closure.
3. **Update everything at once** when something changes: profile, website, listings.
4. **Keep a list** of every listing and its login.
5. **Limit who manages the profile**, and make sure you're the owner.

## The aha: your website is your evidence

When Google sees conflicting information, your own website is one of the places it can check. If your website clearly shows your current hours, phone number and address, it supports what you've put on your profile.

So when something changes, update your website at the same time as your profile. An out-of-date contact page can undo the corrections you've made elsewhere.

## Special cases

### Moved premises

Update the address in your profile, rather than creating a new profile. Then update your website and listings.

### Changed phone number

Keep the old number diverting for a while if you can, while listings catch up.

### Closed for a while

Use the temporarily closed setting, rather than deleting the profile, then reopen when you're back.

### Duplicate listings

If there are two listings for your business, don't ignore it. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) allow one profile per business location. Report the duplicate or ask Google support to merge or remove it.

My [Google Business Profile guide for tradespeople](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/) covers setting up and maintaining the rest of your profile.

## A quick checklist

1. Profile claimed and verified, with you as owner?
2. Every detail correct, including status and special hours?
3. Website showing identical details?
4. Old directory listings corrected?
5. Monthly check in your diary?
