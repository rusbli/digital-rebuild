When your website appears in Google, a searcher sees two things: a blue title and a short description underneath.

That's your advert. It decides whether they click on you or on the result below.

Yet on most small business websites, the titles say "Home", "Services" or just the business name, and the descriptions are whatever Google could scrape together.

Fixing them is one of the quickest, cheapest improvements you can make. Here's how.

> **Quick answer:** Write a unique title for every page that says what the page offers and, for local businesses, where: "Boiler installation in Barry and Penarth | Smith Heating". Put the most important words first and keep it to roughly 50 to 60 characters. Write a description of about 140 to 160 characters that makes a specific promise and gives a reason to click. Make sure both match what's actually on the page.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What makes a good title for a small business page?
- Does the description affect ranking?
- Why does Google sometimes ignore my title?

## What these are, and where they appear

- **The page title** (sometimes called the title tag) is set in your website's code or settings. It appears in browser tabs and is one of the main sources Google uses for the blue link in search results.
- **The meta description** is a short summary, also set in your settings. Google may show it under the link.

If you use a website builder, both usually live in each page's SEO settings. If someone built your site, ask them where to change them, or ask them to change them for you.

## Writing a good title

Here's the first question. A good small business page title:

1. **Says what the page is about**, in the words customers search.
2. **Says where**, for local services.
3. **Includes your business name**, usually at the end.
4. **Is unique** to that page.
5. **Puts the important words first**, in case it's cut off.

### Examples

| Weak title | Better title |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Electrician in Barry and the Vale of Glamorgan \| Jones Electrical |
| Services | Boiler servicing and repairs in Penarth \| Smith Heating |
| About us | About Dave Smith, Gas Safe heating engineer in Barry |
| Gallery | Kitchen and bathroom projects in Cardiff \| Evans Builders |

Google's guidance on [title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) recommends titles that are descriptive and concise, avoiding vague text like "Home" and avoiding stuffing in repeated keywords.

## Writing a good description

Here's the second question. Google says it mainly uses page content to create the snippet under a result, and may use your meta description if it describes the page better. Its guidance on [snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) is clear that a description's job is to summarise the page for searchers.

So the description isn't really about rankings. It's about persuading the person reading it.

A good description:

- **Makes a specific promise**: what they'll find or be able to do.
- **Includes a detail that sets you apart**: fixed prices, same-day callouts, a free check.
- **Matches the page.** Don't promise something the page doesn't deliver.

### Example

> Boiler servicing in Penarth and Barry from a Gas Safe registered engineer. Fixed prices, reminders when your next service is due, and same-week appointments.

Specific, honest and easy to act on.

## Why Google sometimes ignores your title

Here's the third question. Google may rewrite a title if it thinks something else better describes the page. Common reasons include:

- The title is **vague**, like "Home".
- It's **very long** or stuffed with keywords.
- It **doesn't match the page**.
- **Lots of pages share the same title**.

The fix is the same in every case: clear, accurate, unique titles. They're far more likely to be shown as you wrote them.

## The aha: find the pages that need it most

You don't need to rewrite every title at once. Google Search Console shows you where to start.

Open the Performance report and look at pages, or searches, with lots of impressions but few clicks. Those are pages Google is already showing, but people aren't choosing. Better titles and descriptions there can bring more clicks without any change in ranking.

My [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-for-small-business/) shows exactly where to find this.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **The same title on every page.**
- **Just your business name**, with nothing about what you do.
- **Keyword stuffing**: "Plumber Barry, Plumbers Barry, Barry Plumbing, Cheap Plumber Barry".
- **Promises the page doesn't keep.**
- **Forgetting new pages** when you add them.

If you're writing pages for different towns, keep each title genuinely different and the page genuinely useful. My guide on [whether you need a page for every town](/blog/location-pages-for-every-town/) explains why.

## A quick routine

1. List your main pages: home, each service, about, contact.
2. Write a unique title for each, with what, where and your business name.
3. Write a specific description for each.
4. Check Search Console after a month for changes in clicks.
5. Repeat for any page with high impressions and low clicks.
