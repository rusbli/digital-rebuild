People rarely hire a personal trainer on a whim. Usually they've been thinking about it for months. They're a bit nervous: about cost, about whether they'll be judged, about whether it'll work for them.

So when they finally look at your website, they're asking quiet questions. Is this person right for someone like me? Will I feel comfortable? What does it cost? What happens in the first session?

Answer those clearly, and your website will do much of the persuading before you meet.

> **Quick answer:** A personal trainer's website should say clearly who you help and how you train: gym, home, outdoors or online. Show your packages and prices, your genuine qualifications and insurance, and what happens in a first session. Use real photos and genuine client feedback (with permission), avoid hype and promises of specific results, and make booking a first session easy.

Three questions we'll answer:

- How do I show I'm right for a particular person?
- How should I present packages and prices?
- How do I show results honestly?

## Say who you help

Here's the first question. "Personal training for everyone" is hard to connect with. "Strength training for women over 40 in Penarth" or "Getting back to fitness after injury, in Barry and online" speaks directly to someone.

You can still work with lots of people. But leading with the clients you help most makes the right people feel "this is for me".

Also say **how** you train:

- In a gym (which one?).
- At clients' homes.
- Outdoors.
- Online.

## Answer the nerves

Many potential clients worry about:

- **Being judged.** Say plainly that you work with all fitness levels, if you do.
- **What happens first.** Explain your first session: a chat, goals, any health questions, a gentle start.
- **Commitment.** Do they have to sign up for months?
- **Health conditions.** Explain how you handle them, and when you'd suggest checking with a GP first.

A short "Your first session" section does a lot of work here.

## Packages and prices

Here's the second question. Lay them out clearly:

| Package | What's included | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single session | One hour, at gym or home | Your price |
| Block of sessions | Several sessions, often at a lower rate per session | Your price |
| Online coaching | Programme, check-ins, support | Your price |

Explain what's included, how long blocks last, and your cancellation policy. My guide on [whether to show prices on your website](/blog/should-you-show-prices-on-your-website/) explains why clear prices tend to bring better enquiries.

## Qualifications and insurance

List the qualifications you genuinely hold, and any professional memberships or registrations, with details customers can check. Mention your insurance. Never claim a qualification you don't have.

## Showing results honestly

Here's the third question. Before-and-after photos and client stories can be persuasive, but they need care:

- **Only with clear permission** from the client.
- **Only genuine results.** No edited photos, no borrowed images.
- **No promises.** "Lose a stone in a month" sets expectations you can't guarantee. Results vary by person.
- **Focus on how people feel**, too: more energy, less pain, more confidence.

Client feedback in their own words often works better than dramatic photos. My guide to [showing reviews on your website](/blog/show-google-reviews-on-your-website/) covers how to present them.

## Your about page matters

Clients are choosing a person, not a service. A warm, honest about page, with a friendly photo, why you became a trainer and how you work, is often what tips the decision. My guide to [writing an about page](/blog/about-page-for-small-business/) covers it.

## Make booking easy

- A clear button to book a first session or consultation.
- Online booking, if you have set session lengths. My guide to [online booking for small businesses](/blog/online-booking-for-small-business/) covers choosing a tool.
- A phone number and WhatsApp option for people who'd rather ask a question first.

## The aha: lower the first step

The biggest barrier for most potential clients isn't price. It's taking the first step.

So make that step small and safe: a free chat, a consultation, or a single session with no commitment. Say what happens in it, how long it lasts, and that there's no pressure. A small first step gets more people through the door, and the right ones will stay.

## A quick checklist

1. Who you help and how you train, clear at the top?
2. A "Your first session" section?
3. Packages and prices laid out clearly?
4. Genuine qualifications and insurance listed?
5. Honest, permitted client feedback, with no promises?
6. A small, easy first step to book?
