Your homepage isn't read like a book. Nobody starts at the top, reads every word and arrives at the bottom ready to call.

People skim. They look for the answer to whatever question brought them, and if they can't find it quickly, they go back to Google and click the next result.

So the job of a homepage is simple to describe and harder to do: answer the questions visitors have, in the order they have them.

> **Quick answer:** Put these on your homepage, in this order: a headline saying what you do and where, with a contact button; a short list of your main services; proof you can be trusted; how working with you goes; and a clear way to get in touch. Keep each section short. Link to separate pages for the detail.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What should the headline actually say?
- How much detail belongs on the homepage, and how much on other pages?
- Why does the order matter so much?

## Why the order matters

Here's the third question first, because it shapes everything else.

A visitor arriving at your homepage is quietly asking a series of questions, roughly in this order:

1. Am I in the right place? (What is this, and is it near me?)
2. Can they do what I need? (Services.)
3. Can I trust them? (Proof.)
4. What happens if I get in touch? (Process, price.)
5. How do I get in touch? (Contact.)

If your homepage answers question 4 before question 1, people leave before they get there. Put the sections in the same order as the questions, and visitors feel the page is reading their mind.

## Section 1: the headline that does the work

This is the top of the page, the part you see before scrolling. It's the most valuable space on your whole website.

It needs three things:

- **A headline that says what you do and where.** "Electrician in Barry and the Vale of Glamorgan" or "Wedding cakes made to order in Cardiff".
- **One supporting line** that says who you help or why you're different. "Rewires, fuse boards and EV chargers for homes across South Wales. Fixed quotes, tidy work."
- **One main button**, plus your phone number. "Get a free quote", "Book a consultation" or whatever your main next step is.

Here's the first question answered. The headline should describe, not impress. Slogans like "Excellence in every detail" or "Your vision, our passion" sound nice, but they don't tell a stranger what you do. Save the personality for later on the page.

A simple test: cover everything except the headline and show it to someone who's never heard of you. If they can tell you what you do and where, it works.

## Section 2: your main services

Next, show what you do. Keep it short:

- Three to six main services, each with a name and one or two sentences.
- A link from each one to its own page, if you have one.

Here's the second question. The homepage gives a summary. The detail belongs on separate pages. If you do boiler installations, a short card on the homepage says so and links to a full boiler installation page, which covers makes, process, guarantees and prices.

That keeps the homepage easy to skim, and gives Google separate pages to match against searches for each service.

## Section 3: proof you're trustworthy

Now the visitor knows what you do. Next they're asking: "Are you any good, and can I trust you?"

Answer with evidence, not adjectives:

- **Reviews.** A few genuine reviews, with first names and areas, and a link to your Google reviews.
- **Examples of your work.** Photos of finished jobs or screenshots of projects.
- **Accreditations you genuinely hold.** Trade bodies, registrations, insurance.
- **A real person.** A photo of you or your team, and a sentence about who you are.

"We pride ourselves on quality" proves nothing. A photo of a finished job and a review from a customer in Penarth proves a lot.

## Section 4: how it works

Many people hesitate to get in touch because they don't know what happens next. Will they be pressured? Will it cost money to ask?

A short "how it works" section removes that worry:

1. Get in touch by phone or the form.
2. We visit, or have a quick call, and give you a fixed quote.
3. We agree a date and do the work.
4. You pay when you're happy.

Three or four steps is plenty. If the first step is free, say so.

If your pricing is simple, this is a good place for a short summary, or a link to a pricing page. Knowing roughly what something costs makes people much more comfortable getting in touch.

## Section 5: the contact section

End with a clear way to get in touch:

- Your phone number (tappable on mobile).
- A short form, with as few fields as you can manage.
- Your service area and hours.
- What happens next: "I'll reply within one working day."

And don't make people scroll all the way down for it. The contact button in section 1 should jump straight here, and it's worth repeating a button after the services and proof sections too.

## Things that don't earn a place on the homepage

Every section pushes the next one further down. Some common ones aren't worth the space:

- **Long welcome paragraphs.** "Welcome to our website" tells visitors nothing.
- **Your full company history.** Give it a line, and put the rest on the about page.
- **Sliders.** Visitors rarely wait for the next slide, so most of the messages never get seen.
- **News from three years ago.** If you won't keep it updated, leave it off.
- **Everything you've ever done.** Lead with the services that bring in the work you want.

## Check it on your phone

Most of your visitors will see your homepage on a phone, so test it there. Read it top to bottom with your thumb, as a customer would, and ask:

1. Do I know what this business does and where within five seconds?
2. Can I see how to contact them without scrolling?
3. Can I find their services quickly?
4. Is there real proof they're trustworthy?
5. Do I know what happens if I get in touch?

If any answer is no, that's the section to work on first.
