You want more customers from Google. There are two main ways to get them.

You can **pay** to appear at the top, with Google Ads. Or you can work to appear in the **unpaid** results, which is what SEO (search engine optimisation) is about.

People argue endlessly about which is better. The real answer depends on your business, your budget and how quickly you need work.

> **Quick answer:** Google Ads can bring visitors quickly, but you pay for every click and the visits stop when you stop paying. SEO, including your Google Business Profile, is slower to build but keeps working without paying per click. Many small businesses start with the free foundations (a good Business Profile and a clear website), then test a small, tracked ad budget if they need work faster. Fix your website's ability to turn visitors into enquiries first, or both will waste money.

Three questions we'll answer:

- How do the two actually differ?
- Which should I start with?
- What makes either one work?

## How they differ

Here's the first question.

| | Google Ads | SEO (organic search) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where you appear | Sponsored results, usually marked as such | Unpaid results and the map listings |
| Cost | You pay when someone clicks (or by other bidding methods) | Your time, or paying someone; no cost per click |
| Speed | Can bring visits quickly once set up | Builds up over time |
| When you stop | Visits from ads stop | Visibility usually lasts a while |
| Control | Choose searches, areas, times and budget | Influence, but not control, over where you appear |

Google is clear that you can't pay for a better position in its unpaid local results. Its help page on [local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says so directly. Ads and organic results are separate systems.

## Which should you start with?

Here's the second question. For most local small businesses, I'd suggest this order:

### 1. The free foundations

- A complete, accurate **Google Business Profile**, with genuine reviews. My guide to [showing up in near-me searches](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-near-me-searches/) covers this.
- A **website that clearly says what you do and where**, with a page for each main service.
- The **SEO basics** done properly. My guide on [checking your web designer did the SEO basics](/blog/has-my-web-designer-done-the-seo/) has ten checks.
- **Tracking**, so you can see where enquiries come from.

These cost little or nothing beyond your website, and help with both ads and SEO.

### 2. Then decide

- **If you need work quickly**, or you're in a very competitive area, a small, carefully tracked Google Ads budget can make sense.
- **If you can wait**, and want long-term visibility, keep investing in your website, your profile and helpful content.
- **Many businesses do both.**

## What makes Google Ads work

Here's the third question, for ads first:

- **Know what a customer is worth.** If an average job brings in £X profit, you know how much you can afford to pay to win one.
- **Track enquiries, not clicks.** Set up tracking for form submissions and calls. My guide to [Google Analytics for small businesses](/blog/google-analytics-for-small-business/) explains how.
- **Target carefully.** Limit ads to the areas you serve and the services you want.
- **Send people to the right page.** An ad for boiler repairs should land on your boiler repairs page, not your home page.
- **Start small and test.** Increase spending only on what's proven to bring in work.

Without tracking, it's very easy to spend money on clicks without knowing whether any became customers.

## What makes SEO work

- **A complete Business Profile** and steady genuine reviews.
- **Clear service pages** that answer customers' questions.
- **Helpful content** that matches what people search for, like guides and FAQs.
- **Consistency** of your business details across the web.
- **Patience.** Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says some changes take hours and others months to have an effect.

## The aha: both fail on a website that doesn't convert

Ads and SEO both do the same thing: bring people to your website. If your website then fails to turn them into enquiries, both are wasted.

With ads, that waste is easy to see: you're paying for every click. With SEO, it's hidden, but just as real.

So before spending on either, make sure your website makes it obvious what you do, shows why you're trustworthy, and makes getting in touch easy. Doubling the visitors to a site that doesn't persuade anyone just doubles the people who leave.

## A simple decision guide

1. Is my Google Business Profile complete and reviewed? If not, start there.
2. Does my website clearly turn visitors into enquiries? If not, fix that.
3. Do I need work in the next month? If yes, test a small, tracked ad budget.
4. Am I building for the next year or two? Keep investing in SEO and content.
5. Am I tracking where enquiries come from? If not, set that up before spending more.
