QR codes had a slow start, then everyone learned to use them for menus and tickets. Now most people know what to do when they see one: point the camera, tap the link.

For a small business, that makes printed material much more useful. A flyer, a van, an invoice or a business card can take someone straight to the exact page you want, without typing anything.

The trick is sending them to the right place.

> **Quick answer:** Use QR codes on printed material to take people to the most useful page for that moment: your Google review link on invoices, a specific service or offer page on flyers, your contact or booking page on vans and signs. Make the code large enough to scan from where people will be, test it before printing, and prefer codes that link straight to your own web address so they don't stop working.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Where should QR codes go?
- Where should each one send people?
- What mistakes waste them?

## Where QR codes work well

Here's the first question.

| Place | Good destination |
| --- | --- |
| Invoices and receipts | Your Google review link |
| Business cards | Your contact page or website |
| Flyers and leaflets | A page about the specific service or offer on the flyer |
| Van livery | Your contact or quote page |
| Shop window or counter | Booking, menu or reviews |
| Quotes | A page explaining your process or guarantees |

## Send people to the right page

Here's the second question. The most common mistake is linking every QR code to the home page.

Think about the moment. Someone holding your invoice has just had a job done; the most useful thing is to leave a review. Someone holding a flyer about boiler servicing wants to know about boiler servicing, not everything you do.

So:

- **Invoices**: your Google review link. Google lets you create a review link and QR code from your Business Profile. My guide to [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) explains how to ask.
- **Service flyers**: the matching service page. My guide to [writing a services page](/blog/services-page-that-gets-enquiries/) covers what that page should include.
- **Vans and signs**: a short, simple contact or quote page.

Whatever page it is, it'll be opened on a phone, so make sure it works well on mobile. My [mobile-friendly checklist](/blog/mobile-friendly-website-checklist/) takes ten minutes.

## Static or dynamic?

- **Static QR codes** contain the web address directly. They work forever, as long as the page exists.
- **Dynamic QR codes** go through a service that redirects to your page. You can change the destination later, but if the service closes or you stop paying, the code may stop working.

For most small businesses, a static code linking to your own web address is simplest and safest. If you want flexibility, link to a page on your own website that you can update, rather than relying on a third-party redirect.

## Mistakes that waste QR codes

Here's the third question.

- **Too small to scan**, especially on vans and signs.
- **No context.** Add a few words: "Scan to leave a review" or "Scan for a free quote".
- **Untested.** Always scan it yourself from a realistic distance before printing hundreds.
- **Broken destination.** A code to a page you later delete is printed forever.
- **Codes that expire** because they rely on a paid redirect service.
- **Poor contrast**, like a pale code on a busy background.

## The aha: QR codes measure your print

When a QR code goes to a specific page, you can see visits to that page in your website's analytics.

So a QR code on a leaflet tells you, roughly, whether the leaflet worked. If you use different pages, or slightly different web addresses, for different leaflets or areas, you can compare them.

That turns printed marketing from a guess into something you can measure.

## A quick checklist

1. Each code links to the most useful page for that moment?
2. A few words explaining what scanning does?
3. Large enough to scan from where people will be?
4. Tested before printing?
5. Linking to your own web address, not a redirect that might expire?
6. The destination page works well on a phone?
