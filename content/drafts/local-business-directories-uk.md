Once you've got a website and a Google Business Profile, you'll start getting calls and emails offering to list you in directories.

"Get listed in 100 directories for £99."

"Your free listing is ready, just upgrade to Premium."

Some directories are genuinely useful. Some do little. A few are best avoided. Here's how to tell them apart without wasting money.

> **Quick answer:** Focus on a small number of listings that real customers use or that are clearly relevant: the big map and search platforms, your trade body's directory, local business groups and any directory that already sends you work. Keep your name, phone number and area identical everywhere. Avoid mass-submission services and don't pay for a listing until you can see it bringing in enquiries.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Which listings actually matter?
- Do directories help with Google?
- When is paying for a listing worth it?

## The listings that matter most

Here's the first question. Start with the platforms most people use to find businesses:

### Google Business Profile

The most important by a long way, for most local businesses. My [Google Business Profile guide for tradespeople](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/) covers setting it up.

### Apple Business Connect

Lets you manage how your business appears in Apple Maps, which many iPhone users use by default. Apple describes [Business Connect](https://www.apple.com/uk/business/connect/) as a free tool.

### Bing Places for Business

Covers Bing search and maps. [Bing Places](https://www.bing.com/forbusiness) is free, and you can import your details from your Google Business Profile.

### Your trade or professional body

If you're registered with a trade body or scheme that has a public "find a member" directory, make sure your listing is accurate. Customers who search those directories are often ready to hire.

### Local business groups and chambers

Local business networks and community directories can bring genuine local customers, and they're relevant to your area.

## Do directories help with Google?

Here's the second question. A little, in the right circumstances.

Google's help page on [local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) mentions that prominence, how well known a business is, draws on information from across the web, including links and directories.

That doesn't mean "more listings equals higher ranking". A few relevant, accurate listings on reputable sites are worth far more than hundreds of low-quality ones. And Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) warn against creating links mainly to manipulate rankings, which is what bulk directory services often do.

The bigger benefit is usually simpler: real customers find you there, and see the same accurate details wherever they look.

## Consistency matters more than quantity

Imagine a customer finds three listings for your business with three different phone numbers. Which one do they call? Do they trust any of them?

Use exactly the same details everywhere:

- **Business name**, exactly as on your signs and invoices.
- **Phone number.**
- **Address or service area.**
- **Website address.**
- **Opening hours.**

Keep a simple list of every listing you've created, with logins, so you can update them all when something changes.

## When is paying worth it?

Here's the third question. Some directories, particularly trade-specific ones, offer paid listings, leads or membership. Some customers genuinely use them to find tradespeople.

Before paying:

1. **Start with the free version**, if there is one.
2. **Track where enquiries come from.** Ask every new customer how they found you.
3. **Only pay if a directory clearly brings in work** that covers the cost.
4. **Check the contract**: length, notice period and automatic renewal.

Treat paid listings like any other advertising spend: measure them, and stop paying for the ones that don't work.

## What to avoid

- **Mass-submission services** promising listings on hundreds of directories. Many of those sites are low quality and nobody uses them.
- **Unsolicited calls** pressuring you to renew or upgrade a listing "before it's removed".
- **Directories completely unrelated** to your trade or area.
- **Listings you can't edit later.** If your details change, you need to be able to update them.

## The aha: start with the directories that already know you

You probably already appear in directories you've never claimed. Data gets copied between sites, and old details (an old phone number, a previous address) can linger for years.

Search for your business name and phone number on Google. Look at the directories that come up. Claim those listings and correct them. That's often more valuable than creating new ones.

## A simple plan

1. Get your Google Business Profile complete and accurate.
2. Claim Apple Business Connect and Bing Places.
3. Update your trade body or scheme listing.
4. Search for your business name and claim any existing listings with wrong details.
5. Add a few relevant local directories.
6. Keep a list of every listing, and update them all when anything changes.
