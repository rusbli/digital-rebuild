Here's something most small business owners don't realise: the about page is often one of the most visited pages on a small business website.

People read the home page, check the services, and then click "About" to answer one last question: "Who are these people, and can I trust them?"

Yet most about pages are an afterthought. A paragraph of "Established in 2015, we pride ourselves on quality and customer service" and nothing else.

Here's how to write one that does its job.

> **Quick answer:** A good about page introduces the real person or people behind the business, with a photo. It says what you do and who for, why you work the way you do, and any genuine qualifications or accreditations. It mentions where you're based, includes a little personality, and ends with a clear way to get in touch. Keep it a few hundred words.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Why do people click "About" at all?
- What makes an about page feel trustworthy rather than salesy?
- "I" or "we"?

## Why people visit your about page

Here's the first question. By the time someone clicks "About", they're usually interested. They've seen what you offer. Now they want to know who they'd actually be dealing with.

They're asking:

- Is this a real person or a faceless company?
- Do they know what they're doing?
- Will they be easy to deal with?
- Are they local?

Your about page should answer those, and then point them to the next step.

## The structure that works

### 1. A photo of you

A real photo, not a stock image or just a logo. It doesn't need to be professional. A clear, friendly shot of you at work is ideal.

### 2. Who you are, in a sentence

"I'm Dave, a Gas Safe registered heating engineer based in Barry."

Straight in. No "Welcome to our about page".

### 3. What you do and who for

A couple of sentences on the work you do and the customers you help. "I mainly fit and service boilers for homeowners across the Vale of Glamorgan, plus some landlords who need yearly safety checks."

### 4. Why you work the way you do

This is the part that makes an about page memorable. What do you do differently, and why?

"I always send a written quote before starting, because I've seen too many people surprised by the final bill."

One or two honest reasons like this say more than any list of values.

### 5. Experience and credentials

Qualifications, registrations, accreditations, years in the trade. Only what's true, and with registration numbers where customers can check them.

### 6. Where you're based

Your town and the areas you cover. For local businesses, this matters to customers and to Google.

### 7. A touch of personality

One or two lines about life outside work: a local football team, your dog, the fact you've lived in Penarth all your life. It's not essential, but it makes you human and memorable.

### 8. The next step

End with a clear call to action: "Get a free quote" or "Call me on...". Someone who's read your about page is often ready to get in touch. Don't leave them at a dead end.

## Trustworthy, not salesy

Here's the second question. The about pages that build the most trust share a few habits:

- **They're specific.** "Fitted over a hundred bathrooms" (if true) beats "years of experience".
- **They're honest about scale.** If you're a one-person business, say so. Lots of customers prefer it.
- **They avoid empty claims.** "Passionate", "dedicated" and "committed to excellence" mean nothing on their own.
- **They show, not tell.** A photo of your van and a sentence about how you work beats a paragraph of adjectives.

## "I" or "we"?

Here's the third question. If it's just you, write in the first person: "I". It's honest, and it's one of your advantages over bigger companies. Customers know exactly who'll turn up.

Using "we" when you're a sole trader can make you sound bigger, but it often feels slightly evasive when the customer then meets just you.

If you have a team, "we" is natural. But still introduce the people, with names and photos if they're happy with that.

## The aha: your about page is a sales page

People often treat the about page as the one place they're allowed to talk about themselves. But visitors don't read it out of curiosity. They read it to make a decision.

So write it with that in mind. Every line should help a visitor feel more confident about getting in touch, and the page should end with an easy way to do it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **No photo**, or a stock photo.
- **A company history that reads like a CV.**
- **Claims you can't back up.**
- **Out-of-date information**, like a team member who left two years ago.
- **No call to action.**

## Where it fits on your website

Your about page works with your home page and your service pages. The home page gives the quick overview, service pages give the detail, and the about page gives the human reassurance. My guides to [what to put on your homepage](/blog/small-business-homepage-what-to-include/) and [how to write a services page](/blog/services-page-that-gets-enquiries/) cover the other two.

## A quick checklist

1. Is there a real photo of you?
2. Does the first line say who you are and what you do?
3. Is there at least one honest reason why you work the way you do?
4. Are qualifications and registrations shown, and checkable?
5. Does it say where you're based and what you cover?
6. Does it end with a clear way to get in touch?

If you can tick all six, your about page is working harder than most.
