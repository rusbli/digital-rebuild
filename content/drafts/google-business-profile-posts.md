Google Business Profiles have a feature many small businesses never use: posts.

They're short updates that appear on your profile. An offer, an event, a recent job, a seasonal reminder. Like a mini social media feed, right where customers are looking.

Are they worth the effort? Sometimes. Here's how to decide, and what to post if you do.

> **Quick answer:** Google Business Profile posts let you add updates, offers and events, with a photo and a button. They're useful for showing recent work, seasonal services and offers to people already looking at your profile. Google hasn't said they directly improve ranking, so treat them as a way to persuade, not a ranking trick. Posting a few times a month is plenty for most small businesses.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What types of post are there?
- What should I post?
- Are they worth the time?

## Types of post

Here's the first question. Google's help page on [creating and managing posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169) describes the main types:

| Type | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| Update | Recent work, news, seasonal reminders |
| Offer | Discounts or special deals, with dates |
| Event | Open days, workshops, pop-ups, with dates |

Each can include a photo and a button, such as "Book", "Call" or "Learn more".

## What to post

Here's the second question. Good posts for small businesses:

### Recent work

"Just finished this porcelain patio in Penarth." A photo and a sentence. It shows you're active and gives a taste of your work.

### Seasonal reminders

- "Book your boiler service before winter."
- "Planning a spring garden project? We're booking design visits now."
- "Christmas cakes now taking orders."

### Offers

If you genuinely run one, with clear dates and terms.

### Events

Open days, workshops, markets, pop-ups.

### Answers to common questions

"How long does a rewire take? Here's what to expect." With a link to the relevant page on your website.

## What gets posts rejected

Google's help page notes that posts must follow its content policies, and that posts including a phone number in the description may be rejected. Use the call button instead.

Avoid:

- Phone numbers in the text.
- Misleading offers.
- Stock photos.
- Content unrelated to your business.

## How long posts last

According to Google's help page, posts older than six months are archived unless they have a date range, such as an offer or event. That's a good reason to post regularly rather than once.

## Are they worth the time?

Here's the third question. Be realistic. Google hasn't said that posting improves your ranking. The main benefits are:

- **Persuasion**: people viewing your profile see recent, real activity.
- **Information**: seasonal services and offers are visible right where customers are deciding.
- **Freshness**: a profile with recent posts and photos looks active.

If you're already taking photos of your work, posting one to your profile takes a minute. That's usually worth it. If it means hours of extra work each week, it's probably not your best use of time.

## The aha: reuse what you already have

You don't need to create special content for your profile. Use what you already have:

- The photos you take after each job. My guide to [Google Business Profile photos](/blog/google-business-profile-photos/) covers what to photograph.
- The questions customers ask you, answered briefly.
- Your seasonal reminders, which you'd send customers anyway.

One photo, one sentence, one button. Done.

## A simple routine

1. After a good job, take a photo.
2. Once a week or fortnight, add one as an update, with a sentence and a button.
3. Add seasonal reminders a month before they're needed.
4. Add offers or events with clear dates, if you run them.

Posts work best on a profile that's complete and accurate.
