"Electrician near me." "Cafe near me open now." "Dog groomer near me."

These searches are gold for a local business. The person isn't researching. They want someone nearby, usually soon.

So it's frustrating when you search the same thing yourself and a business three towns away appears above you.

Here's how Google actually handles "near me" searches, the part you can't change, and the parts you can.

> **Quick answer:** "Near me" searches are mostly answered by Google's map results, which come from Google Business Profiles. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. You can't change distance, but you can improve relevance (accurate categories, services and a clear website) and prominence (genuine reviews and being mentioned around the web). Don't write "near me" on your pages; it doesn't help.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Should I write "near me" on my website?
- Why does a business further away beat me?
- What's the single most useful thing to do this week?

## What happens when someone types "near me"

When someone searches "plumber near me", Google works out roughly where they are and shows nearby businesses. For most local searches, the top of the page is the map box, with a few businesses listed underneath it.

Those listings come from **Google Business Profiles**, not from websites. Your website still matters (it's often what people check before calling), but the map box is driven by your profile.

So if you're missing from "near me" results, the first place to look is your Google Business Profile.

## Don't write "near me" on your pages

Here's the first question. You'll sometimes see websites with headings like "Best plumber near me". It reads oddly to humans, and it doesn't do the job people hope.

"Near me" describes where the **searcher** is. Google already knows that. What it needs from you is what you do and where you do it.

So write naturally:

- "Plumber and heating engineer in Barry and Penarth."
- "We cover the Vale of Glamorgan and west Cardiff."

That tells both customers and Google exactly what they need.

## The three things Google says it looks at

Google's help page on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names three main factors.

**Relevance.** How well your profile matches the search. Categories, services, your description and your website all help Google understand what you do.

**Distance.** How far you are from the searcher, or from the place they mentioned. You can't change this.

**Prominence.** How well known you are. Google says this draws on information from across the web, including reviews and links.

The same page says there's no way to request or pay for a better ranking in these local results.

## Why a business further away can beat you

Here's the second question. Distance matters, but it isn't everything. A business further away can appear above you because:

- Its **categories** match the search more precisely. A "heating engineer" may beat a general "plumber" for a boiler search.
- Its profile is **more complete**: services, hours, photos and description all filled in.
- It has **more genuine reviews**, which feed prominence.
- It's **better known** online: mentioned on local sites, directories and in the news.
- The searcher was actually nearer to them than you think.

That last one is easy to forget. Results change depending on exactly where someone is standing. Searching from your own kitchen doesn't show you what a customer five miles away sees.

## The steps that help most

### 1. Get your Google Business Profile right

This is the foundation. Use your real business name, choose the most specific category, list your services, set your hours and service area, and add real photos. My [Google Business Profile guide for tradespeople](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/) walks through each section, including keeping your home address private if you work from home.

### 2. Build genuine reviews steadily

Ask every customer, at the right moment, with a direct link. Never offer anything in return. My guide to [getting more Google reviews the honest way](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) covers what to say and what Google's rules forbid.

### 3. Make your website say what and where

Your website supports your profile. Make sure it says clearly:

- What you do, in the words customers use.
- Which towns and areas you serve.
- Your business name, phone number and area, matching your profile exactly.

Give your main services their own pages, so Google has something specific to match.

### 4. Get mentioned locally

Prominence grows when real people and sites mention you. Local directories, your trade body's member list, community groups, local news and suppliers' "where to buy" pages all help, as long as the details are accurate.

### 5. Keep it all consistent

If your phone number is different on your website, your profile and an old directory listing, it creates doubt. Pick one version of your name, address and number and use it everywhere.

## How to see what customers actually see

Searching on your own phone at home gives a skewed picture. Try:

- A private browser window.
- Your phone on mobile data, while you're out in different parts of your area.
- Asking a friend in a nearby town to search and screenshot the results.

And check your Google Business Profile's own performance figures, which show how people found you and what they did.

If your website is set up in Google Search Console, the Performance report shows the actual searches people used. My [Google Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-for-small-business/) explains where to find them.

## What won't help

- **Writing "near me" on every page.**
- **Adding town names to your business name on Google.** Google's guidelines don't allow it, and it can get your profile suspended.
- **Creating profiles at addresses you don't actually work from.** That breaks Google's rules too.
- **Buying reviews or swapping them with other businesses.** Fake engagement can be removed.

## The one thing to do this week

Here's the third question. If you do only one thing, open your Google Business Profile and check every section is complete and accurate, with the most specific category you can honestly choose.

Then ask your next five happy customers for a review.

Those two steps cover relevance and prominence, the two factors you actually control.
