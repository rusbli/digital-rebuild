If you run a business in Wales, you've probably wondered about Welsh on your website.

Some customers would appreciate it. Some competitors have it. But a fully bilingual website sounds like double the work and double the cost.

So do you need one? And if you'd like some Welsh, where do you start?

> **Quick answer:** Most private small businesses in Wales aren't required to have a Welsh-language website; the Welsh language standards apply to public bodies and certain other organisations. But adding Welsh can help you connect with Welsh-speaking customers and show local roots. You can start small, with greetings, key headings and contact details, using the Welsh Government's free Helo Blod translation service, and build up from there.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Am I legally required to have Welsh on my website?
- What's the best way to add Welsh without doubling the work?
- Where can I get help with translation?

## Is it a legal requirement?

Here's the first question. For most private small businesses, no.

The Welsh language standards, which set out requirements for using Welsh and are overseen by the [Welsh Language Commissioner](https://www.welshlanguagecommissioner.wales/), apply to public bodies such as councils, and to certain other organisations named in regulations. Most sole traders, shops, trades and small companies aren't covered.

So for most small businesses, Welsh on your website is a choice. If you're a supplier to a public body, though, it's worth checking whether your contract includes any language requirements.

## Why some businesses choose to add Welsh

- **Connecting with Welsh-speaking customers**, especially in areas where Welsh is widely spoken.
- **Showing you're local** and proud of it.
- **Standing out** from competitors who only use English.
- **Being ready for public sector work**, if that's part of your plans.

It's often a goodwill thing rather than a big commercial one, but goodwill matters for local businesses.

## Ways to add Welsh, from small to full

Here's the second question. You don't have to go fully bilingual on day one.

### Level 1: a warm welcome

A Welsh greeting on your home page, Welsh headings alongside English, and bilingual contact details ("Croeso", "Cysylltwch â ni"). Simple, low-cost and noticeable.

### Level 2: key pages in both languages

Your home page, main services and contact page in both languages. This covers what most customers look at.

### Level 3: fully bilingual

Every page in both languages, with a language switch at the top of each page.

If you go for levels 2 or 3, a few practical points:

- **Separate pages for each language** usually work better than mixing both languages on one page, which can get cluttered.
- **A clear language switch** at the top of every page.
- **Keep both versions updated together**, so the Welsh doesn't fall out of date.

## Getting translation help

Here's the third question. The Welsh Government's **Helo Blod** service offers free translation for businesses. According to [Business Wales](https://businesswales.gov.wales/heloblod/), it can translate up to 500 words a month for free, and it also offers a text-checking service for businesses already using Welsh.

500 words a month is enough for greetings, headings, contact details and a short page or two, which makes levels 1 and 2 very achievable over a few months.

For a fully bilingual site, you may need a professional translator for the rest.

Avoid relying on automatic translation for anything customer-facing without having it checked. Welsh speakers will notice awkward or wrong phrasing, and it can undo the goodwill you were trying to build.

## What about the domain?

Some Welsh businesses use a .wales or .cymru domain to show where they're from. These are run by Nominet and are open to anyone. My guide to [choosing a domain name](/blog/choosing-a-domain-name-uk/) compares them with .co.uk.

## The aha: start with what customers see first

If you're adding Welsh gradually, start where people look first: your website's top section, your contact page, your Google Business Profile and your signage. A small amount of Welsh in those places is noticed far more than a fully translated page buried deep in the site.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Machine translation published without checking.**
- **Welsh pages that fall out of date** while the English ones are updated.
- **A language switch that goes to the home page** instead of the same page in the other language.
- **Treating Welsh as decoration**, for example in images where search engines and screen readers can't read it.

## A simple plan

1. Decide which level suits your business and customers.
2. Use Helo Blod for your greeting, headings and contact details.
3. Add Welsh to your home page and contact page.
4. Expand to your main services over the following months.
5. Keep both languages updated together.
