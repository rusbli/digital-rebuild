You've set up your Google Business Profile. Name, category, phone number, service area, photos. Then you hit the wall: "Get verified".

Until your profile is verified, it may not show up publicly the way you want, and you can't manage it fully. So it matters.

Verification has changed over the years. Postcards used to be the norm; now video and other methods are common. Here's how it works, and how to get it right first time.

> **Quick answer:** Open your Business Profile and select the option to get verified. Google offers one or more methods, such as a phone call or text, email, or a video. Google decides which methods are available. For video verification, plan a short continuous recording showing your location or vehicle, your signage or branding, and proof that you manage the business. Make sure your profile details match reality before you start.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Which verification method will I get?
- How do I prepare for a video verification?
- What should I do if verification fails?

## Why verification exists

Google wants to stop fake businesses and people hijacking real ones. Verification is how it checks that the business is real and that you're entitled to manage it.

That's why the process can feel strict. It's also why a verified profile carries more weight.

## Which method will you get?

Here's the first question. Google's help page on [verifying your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242) explains that the methods are determined automatically, based on things like your business type and location.

Methods you might see include:

- **Phone call or text**, with a code sent to your business number.
- **Email**, to an address shown on the verification screen.
- **Video recording**, showing your business.
- **Live video call** with Google support, in some cases.

You can't usually pick a method that isn't offered. If you see more than one option, choose the one you can complete most easily.

## Before you start: check your details

Many verification failures happen because the profile doesn't match reality. Check:

- **Business name** exactly matches your signs and paperwork. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) don't allow extra keywords.
- **Address** is genuinely where you work, or hidden with a service area set if you visit customers.
- **Phone number** is one you can answer, not an automated system.
- **Category** describes what your business is.

Fix anything that's off before you start.

## Preparing for a video verification

Here's the second question. If you're asked to record a video, plan it before you press record.

Google's instructions will tell you what's needed for your profile. Typically, the video should show:

1. **Where you are.** Outside your premises, street signs, nearby landmarks, or, for a service-area business, your branded vehicle and the area you work in.
2. **Your business.** Signage, branded vehicle, equipment or tools of your trade.
3. **That you manage it.** Unlocking the door, accessing your work area, or showing business documents, like an invoice in your business name.

Tips:

- **Record in one continuous take** if that's what's asked. Don't edit the video.
- **Use good daylight.**
- **Keep it steady and unhurried.**
- **Don't show other people's personal information.**

If you work from home and travel to customers, branded vehicles, tools and paperwork become especially important, because there's no shopfront to show.

## What to do if verification fails

Here's the third question. If verification fails or is rejected:

1. **Read any message Google gives you.** It may point to the problem.
2. **Check your profile details again** against Google's guidelines.
3. **Try again if another attempt is offered**, with better evidence.
4. **Use Google's support routes.** The verification help page links to Google's community forum and support team.

Avoid creating a new duplicate profile. It breaks Google's guidelines and can make things harder. If your profile ends up suspended rather than unverified, my guide on [what to do when a Google Business Profile is suspended](/blog/google-business-profile-suspended/) covers the appeal process.

## The aha: verification is easier if your business looks real everywhere

Google isn't only looking at the video or code. A business with a consistent name, phone number and address across its website, its signage and its other listings looks more genuine than one with inconsistent details.

So before verifying, make sure your website shows the same name, phone number and area as your profile. It's the same consistency that helps you rank, as my guide to [showing up in near-me searches](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-near-me-searches/) explains.

## After verification

Once verified:

- **Fill in every section**: services, hours, description, photos.
- **Start asking for reviews.**
- **Add the profile to your website**, and your website to the profile.
- **Limit who can manage it**, and make sure you're the owner.

My [Google Business Profile guide for tradespeople](/blog/google-business-profile-for-trades/) walks through completing each section properly.

## A quick checklist before you start

1. Name, address, phone and category accurate?
2. Signage, branded vehicle or tools available to show?
3. A business document ready, like an invoice or utility bill?
4. Good daylight and a steady phone?
5. Your website showing the same details?
