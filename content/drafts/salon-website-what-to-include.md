Most salon clients have already decided they want a cut, a colour or a treatment. What they're choosing is where.

And they choose fast, usually on a phone, often late at night. They want to see your work, check your prices, and book without ringing up.

If your website lets them do all three in a couple of minutes, you'll win bookings that go elsewhere when a site makes them work for it.

> **Quick answer:** A salon website should make three things easy: seeing your work (photos of real results), checking prices and durations for each treatment, and booking, ideally online with a button on every page. Add your team, location, hours and genuine reviews. If you offer treatments covered by Wales's special procedures licensing, make sure you're licensed and say so.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What makes someone book with one salon over another?
- How should prices be shown?
- Is online booking worth it?

## Show your work first

Here's the first question. For hair and beauty, people choose with their eyes. A gallery of your real results is your most persuasive page.

- **Use your own photos**, of real clients (with their permission).
- **Show variety**: different hair types, lengths, styles and treatments.
- **Use good, natural light.** My guide to [taking website photos with your phone](/blog/photos-for-your-website-with-a-phone/) has simple tips.
- **Show your salon too.** People want to know what the space feels like.

Avoid stock photos and images from product brands that don't show your own work.

## Prices: make them easy to compare

Here's the second question. Salon clients compare prices, so make yours easy to read.

- **List each treatment with price and duration.**
- **Group treatments** sensibly: cuts, colour, treatments, nails, brows and lashes, and so on.
- **Explain price ranges**, for example by hair length or stylist level.
- **Mention consultations or patch tests** where they're needed.
- **Keep it on a normal web page**, not a PDF price list, so it's easy to read on a phone.

My guide on [whether to show prices on your website](/blog/should-you-show-prices-on-your-website/) explains why clear prices usually bring in better enquiries.

## Online booking: usually worth it

Here's the third question. For most salons, online booking is worth having:

- Clients can book any time, including evenings.
- It cuts down on phone calls while you're with a client.
- Reminders can reduce no-shows.
- Deposits can protect you from last-minute cancellations, if your tool supports them.

Put a "Book now" button on every page, especially near prices and treatments. My guide to [online booking for small businesses](/blog/online-booking-for-small-business/) covers what to check before choosing a tool.

Keep the phone option too. Some clients will always prefer to ring.

## Introduce your team

Clients often book with a specific person. A short profile for each stylist or therapist helps:

- A photo.
- What they specialise in.
- Their experience or qualifications.
- Which days they work.

## Location, hours and parking

- Your address and a map.
- Opening hours, including late nights.
- Parking and public transport.
- Accessibility information.

Keep this identical to your Google Business Profile.

## Licensing in Wales

If you offer certain treatments in Wales, there's an extra point to know. Since 29 November 2024, the Welsh Government's [special procedures licensing](https://www.gov.wales/new-licensing-rules-special-procedures) scheme requires practitioners performing tattooing (including semi-permanent make-up), body piercing, electrolysis and acupuncture to hold a licence, with premises approved too.

If you offer any of these, check you're licensed, and consider mentioning it on your website. It's a strong trust signal for clients.

## Reviews and social proof

- **Genuine Google reviews**, linked from your site.
- **Short quotes** from clients, with permission.
- **Your social media**, if you post your work regularly. Link to it, but keep the key information (prices, booking) on your website, which you control.

## The aha: your website is where the decision is made

Lots of salons get noticed on social media. Someone sees a great colour on Instagram and decides they want it.

But then they go looking for practical details: prices, availability, location, how to book. If those are buried in story highlights and comment replies, many people give up.

Your website is where you turn that interest into a booking. Make it the place with all the practical answers.

## A quick checklist

1. Real photos of your work and salon?
2. Every treatment listed with price and duration?
3. A "Book now" button on every page?
4. Team profiles with specialities and days?
5. Location, hours and parking clear?
6. Any required licences in place, and mentioned?
