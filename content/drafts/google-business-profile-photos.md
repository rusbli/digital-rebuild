When someone compares local businesses on Google Maps, what do they look at first?

Often the photos. Before they've read a single review, they've glanced at your pictures and formed an impression: established or not, tidy or not, the kind of place or person they want.

Many profiles have a logo and nothing else. Some have a few blurry photos from years ago. A handful of good, recent photos can make your profile stand out from the businesses around it.

> **Quick answer:** Add your logo, a cover photo, and real photos of your work, your team, and your premises or vehicle. For shops and cafés, include inside and outside shots so people recognise you. Use good light, keep them recent, and add new ones regularly, such as after good jobs. Avoid stock photos, heavy filters and text-covered images.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What should I photograph?
- How often should I add photos?
- What should I avoid?

## Why photos matter on your profile

Your profile is often the first thing a customer sees, before your website. Photos help them:

- **Recognise you**: your shopfront, your van.
- **Judge your work**: finished jobs, products, food.
- **Trust you**: real people, real premises.

## What to photograph

Here's the first question.

### For trades and service businesses

- **Finished work**, especially before-and-after pairs.
- **Work in progress**, tidy and safe.
- **You and your team**, at work.
- **Your branded van.**
- **Your tools or equipment**, if they show the quality of your work.

### For shops, cafés and salons

- **The outside**, so people recognise you when they arrive.
- **The inside**, so they know what it feels like.
- **Products, dishes or treatments.**
- **Your team.**

### For everyone

- **Your logo.**
- **A cover photo** that represents your business well.

My guide to [taking website photos with your phone](/blog/photos-for-your-website-with-a-phone/) has simple tips for light, framing and preparing images. The same photos work for your website and your profile.

## How often to add photos

Here's the second question. There's no fixed rule, but regular, real updates beat a big batch once a year. A simple habit:

- After every good job, take a few photos.
- Once a week or once a month, add the best to your profile.
- Update your cover photo and outside shots if anything changes.

Fresh photos show customers you're active and busy.

## What to avoid

Here's the third question.

- **Stock photos.** Customers can usually tell, and they undermine trust.
- **Heavy filters** that make things look unlike reality.
- **Photos covered in text or adverts.**
- **Customers' homes or people** without their permission.
- **Personal details** like house numbers or car registrations.
- **Blurry, dark or cluttered shots.**

Google's [Business Profile help centre](https://support.google.com/business/) explains its content policies for photos and how to add them.

## Customer photos

Customers can add their own photos to your listing. Usually that's great: real photos from happy customers are persuasive.

Occasionally, a photo is unflattering, irrelevant or breaks Google's policies. If so, you can report it through your profile. The best defence is plenty of good photos of your own, so the overall picture is accurate.

## The aha: your photos should match your website

When someone finds you on Google Maps and then clicks through to your website, they should feel they've arrived at the same business. Same van, same team, same style of work.

Using the same real photos across your profile and website builds that sense of consistency and trust. If your profile shows real work and your website shows stock images, it creates doubt.

## Keep the rest of the profile current too

Photos work best on a profile where everything else is accurate: hours, phone number, services and status. ## A quick checklist

1. Logo and cover photo added?
2. Real photos of your work, team, and premises or vehicle?
3. Outside and inside shots, if customers visit you?
4. No stock photos, heavy filters or text-covered images?
5. New photos added regularly?
6. The same real photos used on your website?
