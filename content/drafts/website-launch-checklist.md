Launch day for a new website is exciting. It's also when little mistakes slip through: a phone number with a digit wrong, a contact form that sends to nowhere, a page still saying "Lorem ipsum".

Your designer will check plenty. But you know your business better than anyone, and some things only you can spot.

Here's a checklist to run through before you go live, and the things to do straight afterwards.

> **Quick answer:** Before launch, check every page's content and contact details, test every form and button on a phone, make sure page titles and descriptions are set, confirm Google is allowed to index the site, add legal information and a privacy notice, and redirect old addresses if you're replacing a site. After launch, submit your sitemap in Search Console, update your Google Business Profile and listings, and test the form again.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What should I check before launch?
- What only I can check?
- What should I do in the first week?

## Before launch: content

Here's the first question, starting with the words.

- [ ] Every page read through, out loud.
- [ ] No placeholder text, like "Lorem ipsum" or "Your text here".
- [ ] Business name spelled consistently.
- [ ] Phone number, email and address correct everywhere.
- [ ] Prices current.
- [ ] Services list accurate.
- [ ] Areas covered listed correctly.
- [ ] Photos are yours, with permission where needed.

## Before launch: does it work?

- [ ] Every link clicked, including the footer.
- [ ] Phone number tappable on mobile.
- [ ] Contact form sent from a phone, and the email arrived (check spam).
- [ ] Booking tool or WhatsApp button tested, if you have them.
- [ ] Site checked on a phone, using mobile data.
- [ ] Speed tested with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).

My [mobile-friendly checklist](/blog/mobile-friendly-website-checklist/) has more detail on testing with your phone.

## Before launch: Google

- [ ] Each page has a unique title and description. My guide to [page titles and descriptions](/blog/page-titles-and-meta-descriptions/) explains what good ones look like.
- [ ] Any "hide from search engines" setting from the test site switched off.
- [ ] A sitemap exists.
- [ ] HTTPS working, with a padlock.
- [ ] Analytics set up, if you're using it.

My guide on [checking your web designer did the SEO basics](/blog/has-my-web-designer-done-the-seo/) has ten checks you can run.

## Before launch: legal and ownership

- [ ] Business details shown, including company details if you're a limited company. My guide to [the legal information your website should show](/blog/website-terms-and-conditions-uk/) covers this.
- [ ] Privacy notice linked from the footer and forms.
- [ ] Cookies checked against the rules.
- [ ] Domain registered in your name.
- [ ] You have the logins to everything.

## Replacing an old site?

- [ ] Every old page address listed.
- [ ] Old addresses redirect permanently to their closest new page.
- [ ] Content that brought in visitors has been kept.

My guide to [moving a website without losing your Google rankings](/blog/moving-website-without-losing-rankings/) covers this in detail.

## What only you can check

Here's the second question. Your designer can test the technical side. Only you can check:

- Whether the prices are right.
- Whether the services described are what you actually do.
- Whether the areas listed are where you actually work.
- Whether the photos are of your real work.
- Whether it sounds like you.

Set aside an hour, and read every page as if you were a customer.

## The first week after launch

Here's the third question.

1. **Submit your sitemap** in Google Search Console.
2. **Update your Google Business Profile** with the new website address, if it's changed.
3. **Update directory listings and social profiles.**
4. **Test the contact form again**, a day or two after launch.
5. **Tell people**: existing customers, your email signature, social media.
6. **Check Search Console** for errors over the following weeks.

## The aha: test as a stranger, not as the owner

You've seen your new website dozens of times during the build. You know where everything is.

So ask someone who's never seen it to find your phone number, request a quote and tell you what you do, on their phone, while you watch without helping. Five minutes of watching a stranger use your site will show you things no checklist can.

## Keep this checklist

Run the "does it work?" section again every few months, and whenever you make changes. Forms break, phone numbers change and prices go out of date. A quick check keeps your website doing its job.
