Roofing has a trust problem that most trades don't.

The customer can't easily see the work. They're told a few tiles have slipped, or the flashing has failed, or the whole roof needs replacing, and they have to take your word for it. Many have heard stories of doorstep traders and inflated bills.

That's why a roofer's website has a specific job: to prove you're honest before you've even climbed the ladder.

> **Quick answer:** A roofer's website should say what you do and where at the top, with a tappable phone number. Show real photos of your work, including what you found and how you fixed it. Explain how jobs run, give honest guidance on cost, and show guarantees, insurance and genuine reviews. Give repairs and new roofs separate pages, and make the quote form short.

Three questions we'll answer:

- How do you prove honesty online?
- Should repairs and new roofs be on separate pages?
- What should you say about cost?

## Start with the leak

When someone has water coming through the ceiling, they want to know quickly:

- Do you do roof repairs?
- Do you cover my area?
- Can you come soon?
- How do I call you?

Put all four on the first screen of your home page. If you offer emergency or same-day repairs, say so honestly, with the days and times you really cover.

## Proving honesty online

Here's the first question.

### Show what you found

Photos of the problem (with the customer's permission and nothing identifying their home) help homeowners understand what's wrong. A photo of a cracked ridge tile or a perished flashing makes the explanation real.

### Show how you fixed it

Before-and-after photos of repairs and replacements. My guide to [taking website photos with your phone](/blog/photos-for-your-website-with-a-phone/) has tips.

### Written quotes

Say clearly that you provide written, itemised quotes, and that you'll explain what's needed and why.

### Guarantees and insurance

If you offer guarantees on your work, say what they cover and for how long. If you hold public liability insurance, say so.

### A real person

A photo of you and your team, your van, and a short paragraph about who you are. Doorstep traders are anonymous. You're not.

### Genuine reviews

Reviews mentioning honesty, tidiness and fair pricing are especially valuable for roofers. Link to your Google reviews.

## Repairs and new roofs: separate pages

Here's the second question. Yes, give them separate pages.

| Service | What the customer wants to know |
| --- | --- |
| Repairs and leaks | How quickly can you come? What does a call-out cost? |
| New roofs and re-roofs | How long will it take? What materials? What guarantee? Rough cost? |
| Flat roofs | Which systems do you use? How long do they last? |
| Chimneys, fascias, soffits and guttering | Can you do it at the same time as other work? |

Each page answers that customer's questions and gives Google a specific page to match their search. My guide to [what a builder's website needs](/blog/builder-website-what-to-include/) covers project pages for bigger jobs, which suit re-roofs too.

## Talking about cost

Here's the third question. You can't price a roof from a website, but you can help:

- **Explain what drives cost**: roof size, access, scaffolding, materials, the condition of what's underneath.
- **Give a call-out or inspection price** for repairs, if you charge one.
- **Give typical ranges** for common jobs, from your own recent work, if you're comfortable.
- **Explain scaffolding**: whether it's included and why it's needed.

Honest cost guidance is itself a trust signal. A roofer who explains how pricing works seems less likely to invent extras.

## The aha: explain the job before you arrive

Many homeowners feel vulnerable when a roofer says, from the top of a ladder, that more work is needed.

A page explaining the most common roof problems in plain English (slipped tiles, failed flashing, blocked gutters, worn felt) helps. Customers arrive with some understanding, so your diagnosis makes sense when you give it. It turns "I have to trust you" into "that's what I read about".

## The quote form

Keep it short:

- Name and phone.
- Postcode.
- Type of roof and problem.
- Option to upload a photo, if your form allows it.

A photo upload can save a wasted trip, and shows the customer you're taking it seriously.

## A quick checklist

1. Repairs, area and phone number on the first screen?
2. Before-and-after photos, including what you found?
3. Written quotes, guarantees and insurance mentioned?
4. Separate pages for repairs and new roofs?
5. Honest guidance on cost and scaffolding?
6. Genuine reviews and a short quote form?
