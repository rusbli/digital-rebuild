Choosing a driving instructor is a slightly unusual decision. Often two people are making it: the learner, and a parent who's paying.

They want different things. The learner wants someone patient who won't make them feel stupid. The parent wants someone qualified, safe and good value. Both want to know if you've got space in your diary.

Your website needs to answer both, quickly, on a phone.

> **Quick answer:** A driving instructor's website should make it easy to see the areas you cover, your prices and block deals, your ADI status, whether you teach manual or automatic, and your availability. Explain what a first lesson involves, show genuine reviews from recent pupils, and make booking or getting in touch easy, including by message.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What do learners and parents each need to see?
- How should prices and availability be shown?
- How do you build trust before the first lesson?

## Two audiences, one website

Here's the first question.

| Learner wants to know | Parent wants to know |
| --- | --- |
| Will you be patient and friendly? | Are you fully qualified? |
| What's the first lesson like? | How much will it cost overall? |
| Manual or automatic? | Is the car safe and dual-controlled? |
| Can you pick me up from school or college? | Do you have availability soon? |

A website that answers both sets of questions, clearly, wins the booking.

## Show your qualifications clearly

Parents in particular want to know whether you're a fully qualified approved driving instructor or a trainee. GOV.UK's [approved driving instructor register guide](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/guide-to-the-approved-driving-instructor-register) explains that instructors display a green badge if they're qualified, or a pink badge if they're a trainee, while giving paid lessons.

Say clearly which you are. Being a trainee isn't something to hide; many learners are happy with a trainee, often at a lower price. What matters is honesty.

## Prices, packages and availability

Here's the second question. Lay out:

- **Hourly or lesson price.**
- **Block bookings** and any discount.
- **Intensive courses**, if you offer them.
- **Test day**: whether you charge for using your car on test day, and how much.
- **Manual or automatic**, and if you offer both.

Then availability. Learners often contact several instructors because many are fully booked. A line like "Currently taking new pupils for weekday afternoons" or "Waiting list for weekends, about four weeks" saves everyone time and builds trust.

Update it regularly. Out-of-date availability is frustrating for everyone.

My guide on [whether to show prices on your website](/blog/should-you-show-prices-on-your-website/) explains why clear prices bring better enquiries.

## Building trust before the first lesson

Here's the third question.

### What the first lesson involves

Nervous learners want to know what happens. "We'll start somewhere quiet, I'll explain the controls, and you'll only drive when you're ready."

### Your car

Make, manual or automatic, dual controls, and a photo.

### Areas and pick-up points

Which towns you cover, and whether you pick up from home, school, college or work.

### Genuine reviews

Reviews from recent pupils, and parents, saying what you were like to learn with. My guide to [showing Google reviews on your website](/blog/show-google-reviews-on-your-website/) covers how to present them.

### Pass rates, carefully

If you show pass rates, make sure they're accurate and explain how they're worked out. Misleading figures damage trust when learners compare notes.

## Make booking easy

Many learners would rather message than phone. Offer:

- A short contact or booking form.
- A WhatsApp button or text option.
- Online booking for existing pupils, if your diary allows. My guide to [online booking for small businesses](/blog/online-booking-for-small-business/) covers choosing a tool.

## The aha: lead with personality, back it with proof

Learners choose instructors they think they'll get on with. A friendly photo, a few honest lines about how you teach, and reviews that mention patience often matter more than anything else.

Then the parent checks the details: qualifications, prices, car. So lead with the human side, and make the proof easy to find just below it.

## A quick checklist

1. Areas and pick-up points clear?
2. ADI status (qualified or trainee) shown honestly?
3. Prices, blocks and test-day costs listed?
4. Manual or automatic, and your car?
5. Current availability, updated regularly?
6. What the first lesson involves, plus genuine reviews?
7. Easy to message or book?
