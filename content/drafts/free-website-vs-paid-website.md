"Why would I pay for a website when I can get one free?"

It's a fair question. Several website builders offer free plans, and the adverts make it look easy: pick a template, add your details, publish.

For some businesses, starting free is genuinely sensible. For others, it ends up costing more than it saves.

Here's what free usually means in practice, and how to decide.

> **Quick answer:** Free website plans usually include basic pages and hosting, but often add the builder's branding to your site and put it on an address containing the builder's name rather than your own domain. They're fine for testing an idea or getting something online quickly. For an established business, your own domain, a professional look and full control usually justify paying, either for a builder plan or a custom site.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What's the catch with free website plans?
- When is free genuinely good enough?
- What's the cheapest sensible step up?

## What "free" usually includes

Free plans from website builders generally give you:

- A template and editor.
- Hosting.
- A web address on the builder's own domain.

They're a real way to get online without spending money.

## The catch

Here's the first question. Free plans make money for the builder by encouraging you to upgrade. That usually means some limits. Typical ones include:

- **The builder's branding** on your site, often an advert or banner.
- **A web address that includes the builder's name**, rather than yourbusiness.co.uk.
- **Limited features** compared with paid plans.
- **Limited control** over things like design, speed or what you can add.

The details vary by provider and change over time, so check the current plan page of any builder you're considering. Wix's [plans page](https://www.wix.com/plans), for example, explains what you get by upgrading.

The web address is the one that matters most. A customer seeing yourbusiness.sitebuilder.com, rather than yourbusiness.co.uk, may wonder how established you are. And you can't print it neatly on a van.

## When free is good enough

Here's the second question. A free site can be the right choice when:

- **You're testing an idea** and don't know if the business will last.
- **You need something online this week**, and will improve it later.
- **Almost all your work comes from word of mouth**, and you just need somewhere people can check you exist.
- **Money is genuinely tight** right now.

In those cases, a free site is much better than nothing. Just go in knowing you'll probably move later.

## The hidden cost of free: moving later

Here's the thing people don't think about. If your business grows and you want a better website, moving off a free builder usually means rebuilding.

Your free site is built inside the builder's system. The design and pages generally can't be lifted out and taken elsewhere. My guide on [Wix, Squarespace or a custom website](/blog/wix-squarespace-or-custom-website/) explains that trade-off in more detail.

And if you've been printing the free address on leaflets and cards, every one of those becomes out of date.

## The aha: register your own domain, even if the website is free

Here's the third question. The cheapest sensible step up isn't a paid website. It's your own domain name.

Even if you start with a free site, registering your own domain now means:

- Your web address is yours, and nobody else can take it.
- You can use it for email straight away.
- When you upgrade or move your website, the address stays the same.

So the address on your van, cards and invoices never has to change, whatever you build behind it. My guide to [choosing a domain name](/blog/choosing-a-domain-name-uk/) covers how to pick one and register it in your own name.

## Free, cheap or custom?

| Option | Up-front | Ongoing | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free builder plan | Nothing | Nothing (but limits) | Testing an idea, getting online quickly |
| Paid builder plan | Little or nothing | Monthly subscription | Doing it yourself, with your own domain |
| Custom-built site | Build fee | Domain, hosting, optional care | Wanting it done properly once, with low running costs and full ownership |

There's no single right answer. Compare them over three years, not one month, as my guide to [what a small business website costs](/blog/small-business-website-cost-uk/) explains.

## Free alternatives that are worth doing anyway

Whatever you decide about a website, these free steps help a local business get found:

- **A Google Business Profile.** Free, and often more important than the website for local searches.
- **Apple Business Connect and Bing Places.** Free listings on other map services.
- **An accurate trade body listing**, if you're a member of one.

## A simple way to decide

Ask yourself:

1. Is this business here to stay?
2. Do customers need to find me on Google, or do they mostly come by recommendation?
3. Will a builder's name in my web address put anyone off?
4. Do I have the time to build and update it myself?

If the business is new and you're not sure, start free, but register your own domain. If it's established and you want new customers, a proper website on your own domain is usually worth paying for.
