A one-page website is exactly what it sounds like. Everything on a single, scrolling page: what you do, your services, a bit about you, some reviews and a contact form.

They're quick to build, cheap, and can look great on a phone. For some businesses, they're all you need.

For others, a single page quietly limits how many customers can find you. Here's how to tell which you are.

> **Quick answer:** A one-page website suits businesses with one main service, customers who mostly find you by name or recommendation, or a tight budget. If you offer several distinct services, cover several areas, or want to be found on Google for different searches, separate pages work better, because each can focus on one thing. Many businesses start with three to five pages and add more over time.

Three questions we'll answer:

- When is one page enough?
- Why do multiple pages help with Google?
- How many pages should I start with?

## When one page is enough

Here's the first question. A one-page site can be a good fit when:

- **You offer one main service**, like a dog walker or a single-product maker.
- **Most customers find you by name**, through recommendations, social media or your van.
- **You need something online quickly and cheaply.**
- **You're testing a new business idea.**

In those cases, a clear, fast single page, saying what you do, where, why you're trustworthy and how to get in touch, is plenty.

## Why multiple pages help with Google

Here's the second question.

Each page on your website can have its own title, its own main heading and its own focus. That lets each one match a different search.

Say you're an electrician who does rewires, EV chargers and fault finding. On a one-page site, all three share one page and one title. With separate pages:

- "EV charger installation in Barry" can match people searching for EV chargers.
- "House rewiring in the Vale of Glamorgan" can match people searching for rewires.
- "Electrical fault finding in Penarth" can match people with a tripping fuse box.

Each page can also answer that customer's specific questions in detail, which helps them decide. My guide to [writing a services page that brings in enquiries](/blog/services-page-that-gets-enquiries/) shows what to put on each.

## How many pages to start with

Here's the third question. A sensible starting point for many small businesses is three to five pages:

1. **Home**: what you do, where, why choose you, how to get in touch.
2. **One page per main service**: one, two or three, depending on what you do.
3. **About**: who you are.
4. **Contact**: all the ways to get in touch, plus your area.

That's the scope of my own [Basic and Starter packages](/#pricing): up to three or five pages. My guide to [what a small business website costs](/blog/small-business-website-cost-uk/) explains how page count affects price.

## When to add more pages

Add a page when you have something genuinely new and useful to say:

- **A new service** you want to promote.
- **A type of customer** with different needs, like landlords versus homeowners.
- **Answers to common questions**, such as guides or FAQs.
- **Real local content** for a town you work in a lot. My guide on [whether you need a page for every town](/blog/location-pages-for-every-town/) explains when that's worth it.

Avoid adding pages just to have more. Thin pages that repeat each other help nobody.

## The aha: a one-page site is often the home page of a bigger site

Here's a useful way to think about it. A good one-page site is really just a good home page: a clear summary of everything you do.

As your business grows, that page doesn't need throwing away. Each section can grow into its own page, with the home page linking to them. The summary stays; the detail moves.

So if you start with one page, ask your designer to build it in a way that makes splitting it into separate pages easy later.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Cramming everything onto one very long page.** If visitors have to scroll forever, a few separate pages would serve them better.
- **Several pages that say the same thing.**
- **Navigation that jumps around a one-page site confusingly.**
- **No contact option until the very bottom.**

## A quick decision guide

- **One main service, mostly found by name?** One page is probably fine.
- **Several services, want to be found on Google for each?** Use separate pages.
- **Not sure?** Start with three to five pages, and add more as you learn what customers search for.
