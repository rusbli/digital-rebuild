Here's an uncomfortable test. Pick up your phone, open your own website, and try to call yourself using only what's on the screen.

How many taps did it take? Did you have to pinch and zoom? Did a pop-up get in the way? Was the phone number even tappable?

Plenty of small business websites look fine on the computer they were designed on, and quietly frustrate everyone who visits on a phone. Which, for many local businesses, is most visitors.

Here's a ten-minute checklist to find the problems, using nothing but your phone.

> **Quick answer:** On your own phone, check that: the top of the page says what you do and where; your phone number is tappable; text is readable without zooming; buttons are easy to tap; nothing scrolls sideways; pop-ups don't cover the page; images load quickly; menus work; and the contact form is easy to fill in and actually sends. Then run Google's PageSpeed Insights for a mobile score.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What should I check, in what order?
- Why does it look fine on my computer?
- What do I do about the problems I find?

## Why it looks fine on your computer

Here's the second question first, because it explains everything else.

A computer screen is wide. A phone screen is narrow and tall. A website has to rearrange itself to fit. When that rearranging isn't done well, things squash, overlap, shrink or disappear.

You also tend to look at your own website on good Wi-Fi, already knowing where everything is. Customers are on mobile data, in a hurry, seeing it for the first time.

## The ten-minute checklist

Here's the first question. Work through these on your own phone, ideally using mobile data rather than Wi-Fi.

### 1. The first screen

Without scrolling, can you see what you do, where you work and how to contact you? If not, that's the first fix.

### 2. Tappable phone number

Tap your phone number. Does your phone offer to call it? If nothing happens, it needs turning into a link.

### 3. Readable text

Can you read everything comfortably without zooming? Small grey text on a white background is a common problem.

### 4. Buttons and links

Are buttons big enough to tap with a thumb? Are links far enough apart that you don't hit the wrong one?

### 5. No sideways scrolling

Scroll down the page. Does anything make the page wobble left and right? Wide tables, images or embedded content are common culprits.

### 6. Pop-ups

Does a pop-up, banner or chat window cover the content? Can you close it easily? Pop-ups that are easy to dismiss on a computer can be impossible to close on a phone.

### 7. Menu

Open the menu. Does it work? Can you close it? Does it include the pages people need?

### 8. Images

Do images load quickly and fit the screen? Are they cropped in a way that still makes sense?

### 9. Contact form

Fill in your own form. Do the right keyboards appear for phone and email? Is it easy? Does the message actually arrive? My guide to [contact forms that people actually fill in](/blog/contact-form-that-gets-filled-in/) covers the details.

### 10. Speed

Run your home page through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and look at the mobile results. My guide on [how fast a website should load](/blog/how-fast-should-a-website-load/) explains what the numbers mean.

## What to do about what you find

Here's the third question. Problems fall into roughly three groups:

| Problem | Typical fix | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone number not tappable, text too small, unclear headline | Content and settings changes | Usually easy |
| Pop-ups, menus, large images, slow loading | Remove or adjust plugins and images | Usually moderate |
| Layout breaks, sideways scrolling, overlapping elements throughout | The theme or build isn't properly responsive | Can mean a rebuild |

Start with the easy ones. Making the phone number tappable and the headline clearer can make a real difference in an afternoon.

If the problems are everywhere and stem from how the site is built, fixing them one by one may cost more than starting again. My guide on [whether your website needs a rebuild](/blog/signs-your-website-needs-a-rebuild/) helps you decide.

## The aha: test the customer's journey, not the page

Most people check whether their website "looks OK" on a phone. That's the wrong test.

The right test is whether a customer can **do** what they came to do: find out what you do, trust you, and get in touch. A page can look fine and still hide the phone number at the bottom, or have a form that doesn't send.

So don't just look. Try to book yourself.

## Google and mobile

Google mainly uses the mobile version of websites for indexing and ranking, which it calls [mobile-first indexing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing). So if important content is hidden or missing on mobile, Google may not see it either.

But the bigger reason is people. Mobile visitors are often the ones ready to call right now. My guide to [websites that get visitors but no enquiries](/blog/website-not-getting-enquiries/) explains how small mobile problems add up to lost work.

## Repeat it regularly

Run this checklist:

- After any change to your website.
- After adding a new plugin, pop-up or tool.
- Every few months, as a habit.

It only takes ten minutes, and it's ten minutes spent seeing your business the way your customers do.
