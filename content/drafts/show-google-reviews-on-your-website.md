You've worked hard to collect Google reviews. They sit on your Business Profile, doing a great job for anyone who finds you on Google Maps.

But many people find you another way: a recommendation, a Facebook post, your van. They land on your website, and there isn't a single review in sight.

Reviews are some of the most persuasive content you have. Here's how to put them to work on your website.

> **Quick answer:** Show a few genuine Google reviews on your home page and relevant service pages, with the reviewer's first name and area, and a link to your full Google reviews. You can quote them yourself or use a widget, but check widgets don't slow your site or add unwanted cookies. Pick reviews that mention specifics, quote them accurately, and keep them fresh.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What's the best way to display reviews?
- Which reviews should I choose?
- What makes reviews less believable?

## Ways to show reviews

Here's the first question.

| Method | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quote reviews yourself, with a link to Google | Fast, simple, you choose the best ones | Needs updating by hand |
| A review widget | Updates automatically | Can slow pages, add cookies or look generic |
| A link or button to your Google reviews | Easy, always current | Takes visitors away from your site |

For most small businesses, a few hand-picked quotes plus a link to all your Google reviews is the best balance. It's quick to load, and visitors can check the rest if they want.

If you use a widget, test your page speed afterwards. My guide on [how fast a website should load](/blog/how-fast-should-a-website-load/) explains how. And check whether it sets cookies, which may affect what you need to tell visitors.

## Which reviews to choose

Here's the second question. The most persuasive reviews are specific.

Compare:

> "Great service, highly recommend."

with:

> "Dan replaced our consumer unit in a day, explained everything clearly and left the place spotless. Fair price, too." Sarah, Penarth

The second tells a new customer exactly what to expect. Look for reviews that mention:

- **The specific service.**
- **Reliability**: turning up on time, finishing when promised.
- **Communication**: explaining things, keeping in touch.
- **Tidiness.**
- **Price and value.**
- **The area**, if they mention it.

## Where to put reviews

- **Home page**: two or three of your best, near the top or right after your services.
- **Service pages**: reviews that mention that particular service.
- **Contact page**: one short review near the form, as a final reassurance.
- **About page**: reviews that mention you by name.

A review about bathroom fitting on your bathroom fitting page is more persuasive than the same review on a general page.

## What makes reviews less believable

Here's the third question.

- **Only perfect reviews.** A wall of identical five-star quotes can look curated. A slightly mixed selection can be more believable.
- **Anonymous reviews.** "J, Cardiff" is fine; "A customer" looks made up.
- **Edited wording.** Never change what a customer said. Shorten if you must, but keep the meaning.
- **Reviews from years ago.** Keep them fresh.
- **Fake reviews.** Never. Apart from being dishonest, they're against Google's rules on its own platform, and customers are good at spotting them.

## The aha: reviews answer objections you can't

There are things you can't say about yourself without sounding like you're bragging: "I'm really tidy", "I always turn up on time", "I'm honest about prices".

Your customers can say them for you, and they're believed.

So think about the worries that stop people from getting in touch with you, then choose reviews that answer them. If people worry about mess, pick a review that mentions tidiness. If they worry about cost, pick one that mentions a fair price.

## A quick checklist

1. Two or three specific reviews on your home page?
2. Relevant reviews on each service page?
3. First names and areas, quoted accurately?
4. A link to all your Google reviews?
5. Reviews refreshed every few months?
6. Page speed checked, if you use a widget?
