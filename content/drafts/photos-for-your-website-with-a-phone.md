A stock photo of a smiling man in a hard hat. A glossy kitchen that obviously isn't in Barry. A handshake in front of a laptop.

Customers have seen these a thousand times. They don't trust them, and they don't remember them.

A real photo of your actual work, even a slightly imperfect one, does far more. It shows what you do, proves you're real and helps customers picture you working on their job.

And you don't need a photographer. The phone in your pocket is enough, if you know a few simple tricks.

> **Quick answer:** Use natural light, clean up the space, hold the phone level and get the whole subject in frame. Take several shots from different angles, including before-and-after pairs. Ask customers' permission and avoid anything that identifies their home. Before uploading, resize photos and save them in a web-friendly format so they don't slow your website down.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What makes a phone photo look professional?
- What should I photograph?
- How do I stop photos slowing down my website?

## The four habits that make the biggest difference

Here's the first question.

### 1. Light

Light is everything. Natural daylight is best.

- Turn off harsh overhead lights if there's good daylight.
- Shoot with the window behind you, not behind the subject.
- Avoid the flash. It flattens everything and creates glare.
- Overcast days are great for outdoor shots; there are no harsh shadows.

### 2. Tidy up first

Two minutes of tidying transforms a photo. Remove tools, dust sheets, bins, cables, boxes and personal items. Wipe surfaces. Straighten towels.

### 3. Keep it level and straight

Wonky photos look amateurish. Turn on your camera's grid lines (most phones have this in camera settings) and line up vertical edges, like door frames and walls, with the grid.

### 4. Get the whole subject in

Step back. Show the whole bathroom, the whole extension, the whole garden. Then take close-ups of the details you're proud of.

## What to photograph

Here's the second question.

### Before and after

The most persuasive photos for most trades. Take the "before" from a spot you can remember, then take the "after" from the same position, at the same angle.

### Work in progress

A few tidy "during" photos show how you work and how you treat people's homes. My guide to [what a builder's website needs](/blog/builder-website-what-to-include/) explains why these are surprisingly persuasive.

### Details

Neat tiling edges, tidy wiring, a well-finished joint. Close-ups that show the quality of your work.

### You and your team

People hire people. A friendly photo of you at work or beside your van belongs on your about page. My guide to [writing an about page](/blog/about-page-for-small-business/) covers what else to include.

### Your van and premises

Shows you're an established business, and helps customers recognise you when you turn up.

## Permission and privacy

- **Ask customers before photographing their home**, and before publishing.
- **Avoid house numbers, car registrations, family photos and anything personal.**
- **Don't photograph people without permission.**

Most customers are happy to say yes, especially if you offer to show them the photos first.

## Preparing photos for your website

Here's the third question. Photos straight from a modern phone are often several megabytes each. Upload a few of those and your page becomes slow, especially on mobile.

Before uploading:

1. **Crop** to remove distractions.
2. **Resize** to roughly the size the photo will be shown. For a large photo across a page, around 1,200 to 2,000 pixels wide is usually plenty.
3. **Save in a web-friendly format**, such as WebP, which keeps quality while reducing file size.
4. **Give it a sensible file name**, like `bathroom-refit-penarth.webp`, rather than `IMG_4837.jpg`.
5. **Write alt text** describing what's in the photo, for people using screen readers: "Walk-in shower with grey tiles and glass screen."

Free image tools can resize and convert photos in a couple of clicks. Or ask your web designer to handle it.

My guide on [how fast a website should load](/blog/how-fast-should-a-website-load/) explains why oversized images are the most common reason small business sites are slow.

## The aha: build a photo habit, not a photo shoot

Most business owners think about photos once, when the website is built, and never again. So the website shows the same five photos for years.

The businesses with the best websites simply take photos at the end of every good job. Thirty seconds, five shots, saved in a folder. After a few months, you have a library of real work to choose from: for your website, your Google Business Profile and your social media.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Stock photos passed off as your work.** It's misleading, and customers often notice.
- **Dark, blurry or cluttered shots.**
- **Huge, unprocessed files uploaded straight from your phone.**
- **Photos showing customers' personal details.**
- **The same old photos for years.**

## A quick routine for every job

1. Take a "before" photo when you arrive.
2. Take one or two tidy "during" photos.
3. When you finish, tidy up, check the light and take three to five "after" photos.
4. Ask the customer's permission to use them.
5. Save them to a folder named by job and date.

Within a few months, your website, Google profile and social pages will look busier, more real and more trustworthy.
