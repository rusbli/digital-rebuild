Painting and decorating is one of those jobs where everybody thinks they could do it themselves, until they try.

That's useful to remember when writing your website. Your customers aren't just paying for paint on walls. They're paying for good preparation, clean lines, no drips on the carpet, and someone who turns up when they said they would.

Your website's job is to show all of that before you've even quoted.

> **Quick answer:** A decorator's website should lead with before-and-after photos and close-ups that show the quality of your finish. Explain how a job runs, especially how you protect furniture and floors and how long it takes. Give clear guidance on pricing, show genuine reviews mentioning tidiness and reliability, and make it easy to request a quote, ideally with photos of the room.

Three questions we'll answer:

- How do you show quality in photos?
- What worries do customers have, and how do you answer them?
- How should prices be shown?

## Show quality, not just colour

Here's the first question. A wide shot of a freshly painted room is nice. But many rooms look fine from across the room. The difference shows up close.

Include:

- **Before-and-after pairs** from the same spot.
- **Close-ups** of cutting-in, skirting, door frames and coving.
- **Preparation**: filled cracks, sanded woodwork, masked edges.
- **Wallpapering**, if you do it, showing neatly matched patterns.
- **Exterior work**, if you do it.

My guide to [taking website photos with your phone](/blog/photos-for-your-website-with-a-phone/) has tips for getting good light and clean shots.

## Answer the worries customers have

Here's the second question. People booking a decorator usually worry about:

### Mess

Explain how you protect furniture and floors, and how you leave the room. "Dust sheets down, furniture covered, everything cleaned up at the end of each day."

### Disruption

How long will it take? Can they use the room in the evenings? Will they need to move furniture?

### Reliability

Will you turn up when you say, and finish when you said?

### Quality

Will it last? What paints do you use? What happens if there's a problem?

A short "How a job works" section answering these removes the hesitation that stops people getting in touch.

## Prices: give people a starting point

Here's the third question. Customers want a rough idea before they ask for a quote. Options that work:

- **A day rate**, with an idea of what can be done in a day.
- **Typical prices** for common jobs, like a single bedroom, a hallway, stairs and landing, or a front door.
- **What's included**: preparation, materials, tidying up.
- **How you quote**: whether you visit, and how quickly quotes arrive.

Use your own real figures. My guide on [whether to show prices on your website](/blog/should-you-show-prices-on-your-website/) explains why some figure is usually better than none.

## Make quoting easy

A short form with:

- Name, phone and postcode.
- Which rooms or areas.
- Interior or exterior.
- When they'd like the work done.
- An option to upload photos of the rooms.

Photos let you give a rough idea quickly, or decide whether a visit is needed.

## Reviews that matter for decorators

The best reviews for a decorator mention tidiness, punctuality, finish and fair price. When you ask customers for reviews, it's natural to say "If you're happy with how we left the place, it would really help if you mentioned it". Never ask for a particular rating, though.

Then show the best on your website. My guide to [showing your Google reviews on your website](/blog/show-google-reviews-on-your-website/) explains how.

## The aha: sell the preparation

Most customers can't tell good paint from cheap paint. But they can understand that preparation is what makes a finish last and look professional.

A short section explaining your preparation (filling, sanding, priming, protecting) with a couple of photos helps customers understand why your quote is what it is. It turns "why are you more expensive?" into "that's why".

## Separate pages for different work

If you do several types of work, give the main ones their own pages:

- Interior painting.
- Exterior painting.
- Wallpapering.
- Commercial work.

Each page answers its own customers' questions, and gives Google something specific to match.

## A quick checklist

1. Before-and-after and close-up photos?
2. A clear explanation of how you protect people's homes?
3. Timescales and disruption explained?
4. Day rates, typical prices or how you quote?
5. Genuine reviews mentioning tidiness and reliability?
6. A quote form with photo upload?
