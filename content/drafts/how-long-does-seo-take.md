"How long until I'm on the first page of Google?"

It's the question everyone asks, and the honest answer ("it depends") is frustrating. So here's a more useful version: what it depends on, what you can realistically expect, and how to tell whether things are moving in the right direction.

> **Quick answer:** There's no fixed timeline. Google says some changes take effect within hours and others take several months. How quickly a small business shows up depends on how competitive the searches are, how established your site and Business Profile are, and how useful your pages are compared with others. Local businesses can often see early progress through their Google Business Profile, while website rankings for competitive searches tend to build over months.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What decides how long it takes?
- What can a small local business expect?
- How do I know it's working?

## What decides how long it takes

Here's the first question.

### How competitive the search is

"Emergency plumber Cardiff" has lots of businesses competing. "Victorian sash window repair Penarth" has far fewer. Specific, local searches usually move faster.

### How established your site is

A brand-new website with no links and no history takes longer than an established site that Google already knows and trusts.

### How good your pages are

Google ranks pages that are useful for the search. A clear, detailed service page answering customers' real questions will do better than a one-line mention.

### Your Google Business Profile

For local searches, the map results come from Business Profiles. A complete profile with genuine reviews can make a difference more quickly than website changes.

### How much you've done

The basics done properly, steady reviews, helpful content and genuine local links all add up over time.

## What a small local business can expect

Here's the second question. Without promising timelines nobody can guarantee, a common pattern looks like this:

| Stage | What often happens |
| --- | --- |
| First weeks | Google finds and indexes your pages. You appear for your business name. |
| Early months | A complete Business Profile and reviews start showing in local results. Some specific searches begin appearing in Search Console. |
| Over the following months | More searches for what you do and where. Clicks and enquiries grow, especially for less competitive, specific searches. |
| Longer term | Steady content, reviews and links build visibility for more competitive searches. |

Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says some changes take effect within hours and others can take months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change worked.

## How to tell it's working

Here's the third question. Don't judge by searching for yourself every day; results vary by person and place.

Instead, look at Google Search Console's Performance report, over three-month periods:

- **Impressions rising**: you're appearing more often.
- **More searches for what you do and where**, not just your business name.
- **Clicks rising.**
- **Enquiries from Google rising**, which you can track with Google Analytics.

My [Search Console guide](/blog/google-search-console-for-small-business/) shows where to find each of these.

## How to speed things up honestly

- **Complete your Google Business Profile** and ask every customer for a review.
- **Give each main service its own clear page.**
- **Target specific searches first**: particular services and places, not broad terms.
- **Earn genuine local links.** My guide to [earning links to a local business website](/blog/how-to-get-links-to-your-local-business-website/) covers how.
- **Keep adding helpful content** that answers real customer questions.
- **Fix the basics**: indexing, speed, mobile, page titles.

## The aha: start where you can win

Many small businesses aim straight for the most competitive search in their area, like "plumber Cardiff", and get discouraged when nothing happens.

It's usually faster to start with specific searches where there's less competition and the searcher is ready to buy: a particular service, in a particular town. Each one you win brings real enquiries, and the visibility you build helps with broader searches later.

## SEO or ads?

If you need enquiries next month, SEO alone may be too slow, and a small, tracked ad budget might fill the gap. My guide to [Google Ads versus SEO](/blog/google-ads-or-seo-small-business/) explains how to decide.

## What to be wary of

- **Guaranteed rankings.** Nobody can guarantee them, and Google's help page on [local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says there's no way to pay for a better local position.
- **Quick tricks**, like buying links or stuffing keywords. They break Google's spam policies.
- **Monthly reports full of jargon** with no change in enquiries.

## A quick plan

1. Complete your Business Profile and start collecting reviews.
2. Make sure each main service has a clear page.
3. Pick three specific searches to focus on.
4. Check Search Console monthly, comparing three-month periods.
5. Keep improving pages and adding helpful content.
