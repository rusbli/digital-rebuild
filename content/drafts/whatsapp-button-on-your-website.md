Some customers love the phone. Some love forms. And a growing number would much rather send a quick message, with a photo, and get a reply when you're free.

For trades especially, a WhatsApp message with a photo of the leaking tap or the cracked tile can be more useful than any form. You can see the problem straight away.

So should you add a WhatsApp button to your website? For many small businesses, yes, as long as you set it up so it doesn't take over your evenings.

> **Quick answer:** A WhatsApp button can bring in enquiries from people who'd rather message than call or fill in a form, and lets customers send photos easily. Add it with a simple click-to-chat link, ideally to a separate business number using the WhatsApp Business app, with greeting and away messages set. Keep your phone number and form too, and mention WhatsApp in your privacy notice.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Who does a WhatsApp button actually help?
- How do I add one simply?
- How do I stop it taking over my life?

## Who it helps

Here's the first question. A WhatsApp button helps:

- **Customers who don't like phone calls.**
- **People at work**, who can message but can't call.
- **Customers with something to show you**: a photo or video of the problem.
- **Evening browsers**, who want to ask a question without waiting until morning.

And it helps you: a photo often lets you give a rough idea or decide whether a visit is needed, saving time.

## How to add one simply

Here's the second question. WhatsApp offers a click-to-chat link in this format:

`https://wa.me/447700900000`

Use your number in international format: country code (44 for the UK), then your number without the leading zero, and no plus sign, spaces or brackets.

Your web designer can turn this into a button. A few tips:

- **Label it clearly**: "Message us on WhatsApp".
- **Put it where contact options appear**: the top of the page, service pages and the contact page.
- **Avoid heavy chat widgets**, which can slow your site. A simple link is enough. My [mobile-friendly checklist](/blog/mobile-friendly-website-checklist/) covers why speed and pop-ups matter on phones.
- **Test it** on your own phone.

You can also add a pre-filled message to the link, so the chat starts with something like "Hi, I'd like a quote", which some people find easier.

## Keeping it manageable

Here's the third question. The worry with messaging is that customers expect instant replies at all hours.

### Use a separate business number

The [WhatsApp Business app](https://business.whatsapp.com/) is designed for small businesses. It lets you keep work separate from personal chats, and set up:

- **Greeting messages** for first-time contacts.
- **Away messages** outside working hours.
- **Quick replies** for common questions.
- **A business profile** with your hours, address and website.

### Set expectations

An away message such as "Thanks for your message. I'm out on jobs, and I'll reply this evening" means customers know when to expect an answer.

### Keep other options

Don't replace your phone number or form. Offer WhatsApp alongside them. My guide to [contact forms people actually fill in](/blog/contact-form-that-gets-filled-in/) explains why giving people a choice catches more enquiries.

## The aha: photos make better first conversations

A form message says "I've got a leak under my sink." A WhatsApp message says the same, with a photo showing exactly what's wrong.

That photo can save a wasted trip, help you bring the right parts, or let you tell the customer straight away that it's a simple job. It makes the first conversation more useful for both of you.

## Privacy

Customers sending messages to your business are sharing personal information. Mention WhatsApp in your privacy notice alongside your other contact methods. My guide to [privacy policies for small business websites](/blog/privacy-policy-small-business-website/) explains what to include.

## A quick checklist

1. Separate business number with WhatsApp Business?
2. Greeting and away messages set?
3. Simple click-to-chat button, clearly labelled?
4. Phone number and form still available?
5. Tested on your own phone?
6. Mentioned in your privacy notice?
