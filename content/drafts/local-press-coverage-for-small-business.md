A mention in the local paper or on a local news website does something an advert can't. It's someone else saying you're worth knowing about.

For a small local business, that matters. It builds trust with people who see it, and an article online can mention or link to your website, which can help people, and Google, find you.

You don't need a PR agency. You need a genuine story and a short, well-aimed email.

> **Quick answer:** Local media want stories that interest local people: milestones, community projects, unusual jobs, local firsts, useful seasonal advice and human stories. Find a genuine angle, write a short pitch with the key facts and good photos, and send it to the right reporter or news desk. Coverage builds trust and can bring mentions and links that help your visibility. Never pay for links meant to manipulate rankings.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What makes a story worth covering?
- How do I pitch it?
- How does coverage help me online?

## What makes a story worth covering

Here's the first question. Local reporters aren't interested in adverts. They're interested in stories their readers will care about. Angles that work:

- **Milestones**: ten years in business, your thousandth job, a new premises.
- **Community**: sponsoring a local team, helping a charity, free work for a community building.
- **People**: taking on an apprentice, a career change, a family business passed down.
- **Unusual jobs**: restoring something historic, an unusual commission.
- **Local firsts**: a new service no one else offers locally.
- **Useful advice**: "how to stop your pipes freezing this week", tied to the weather or season.

Ask yourself: would a neighbour find this interesting if they heard it in the pub? If yes, it's probably a story.

## How to pitch

Here's the second question.

### Find the right person

Look at the local news site and find reporters who cover business or community stories. Many publications list a news desk email.

### Write a short email

- **Subject line**: the story in a few words. "Barry electrician trains three local apprentices."
- **First two lines**: the story, who, what, where, when.
- **A few more details**: why it's interesting, a quote from you.
- **Photos**: good, real photos, with permission from anyone in them.
- **Your contact details**, and when you're available.

Keep it to a few short paragraphs. Reporters are busy.

### Follow up once

If you don't hear back within a few days, a polite follow-up is fine. Then let it go.

## How coverage helps you online

Here's the third question.

- **Trust.** A link to "as featured in" a local news site on your website reassures customers.
- **Prominence.** Google's help page on [local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says prominence draws on information from across the web, including links and articles.
- **Discovery.** People reading the article may visit your site or search for your business.
- **AI search.** Tools that answer questions draw on what's said about you across the web. My guide on [getting mentioned in AI search answers](/blog/get-recommended-by-ai-search/) explains why outside mentions help.

Make sure the article, if it mentions your business, uses your correct name and links to the right website if possible.

## What to avoid

- **Paying for links.** Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) describe buying links to manipulate rankings as link spam. Paid adverts are fine; paying for a link meant to boost rankings isn't.
- **Pure adverts disguised as news.** Reporters will ignore them.
- **Exaggeration.** Stick to facts you can stand behind.

## The aha: seasonal advice is the easiest story

You don't need a big milestone. Local news sites often need practical, timely content: frozen pipes in a cold snap, storm damage after high winds, garden jobs before spring.

If you can offer genuinely useful advice, quickly, when the weather makes it relevant, you become the local expert reporters call. That's coverage you can earn again and again.

## Make the most of coverage

- **Share it** on your website, social media and Google Business Profile.
- **Thank the reporter.**
- **Keep in touch**, so they remember you for future stories.

Combine it with other genuine local mentions, like accurate directory listings. My guide to [business directories](/blog/local-business-directories-uk/) covers which ones are worth claiming.

## A quick plan

1. List three possible stories from your business this year.
2. Find the right reporter or news desk at two local outlets.
3. Write a short pitch for your best story, with photos.
4. Follow up once.
5. Share any coverage on your website and profile.
