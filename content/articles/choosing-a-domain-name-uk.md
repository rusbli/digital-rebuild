Your domain name ends up everywhere. On your van, your invoices, your business cards, your email address and every listing you ever create.

So it's worth ten minutes of thought before you register one, because changing it later is a real nuisance.

The questions people ask most are which ending to choose, whether to stuff it with keywords, and who should actually register it. The last one matters most, so we'll get to it.

> **Quick answer:** Choose a short domain based on your business name that's easy to say out loud and spell. For a UK business, .co.uk is a natural choice; register the .com too if you can and point it at the same site. Avoid hyphens and keyword stuffing. Most importantly, register it in your own name, in an account you control.

Three questions we'll answer:

- Does the domain ending affect Google?
- Should I put words like "plumber" or my town in the domain?
- What's the one mistake that causes trouble years later?

## The radio test

Before anything else, say your domain out loud as if you were reading it to someone over the phone.

"It's smithplumbing dot co dot uk."

Easy. Now try "it's smith hyphen plumbing hyphen and hyphen heating dot co dot uk". Or "S-M-Y-T-H-E, no, with a Y and an E".

If you have to spell it, explain it, or repeat it, customers will get it wrong. And a customer who types the wrong address doesn't reach you.

So the first rule is: short, easy to say, easy to spell.

## .co.uk, .uk or .com?

Here's how the common options compare:

| Ending | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| .co.uk | UK businesses with UK customers; instantly recognisable here | Less familiar to overseas customers |
| .com | Businesses with customers abroad; very widely recognised | Short, good names are often taken |
| .uk | Shorter version of .co.uk | Less familiar to some people, who may type .co.uk out of habit |
| .wales / .cymru | Welsh businesses wanting to show where they're from | Less familiar to customers outside Wales |

Here's the first question. Your domain ending isn't something you need to worry about for Google. What's on your pages, and how useful they are, matters far more. Choose the ending your customers will remember.

If you can afford it, register both your .co.uk and .com, and point the one you don't use at the one you do. That way, a customer who guesses wrong still finds you, and nobody else can register the obvious alternative.

## Keywords in your domain: not worth forcing

Here's the second question. There's an old idea that a domain like "best-cheap-plumber-cardiff.co.uk" helps you rank. It doesn't do enough to be worth the downsides.

Long keyword domains:

- Are hard to say and easy to mistype.
- Look less trustworthy than a real business name.
- Can box you in if you later expand to other services or areas.

A domain based on your business name is usually the better choice. If your business name is very common, adding a single word for your trade or area can help: "jonesroofingbarry.co.uk" is still easy to say.

## Checking the name is free, and safe

Before you register:

1. **Search for the name on Google.** Is another business already using something very similar nearby? Customers could confuse the two.
2. **Check it's not a trademark** of a known brand.
3. **Check social media handles.** It's nice, though not essential, if the same name is free on the platforms you use.
4. **Try typing it on your phone.** Autocorrect sometimes mangles unusual spellings.

## The mistake that causes trouble years later

Here's the third question. The biggest domain mistake isn't choosing the wrong ending. It's letting someone else register it in **their** name.

It happens innocently. A web designer, a relative or a former business partner registers the domain to save you the bother. Years later, there's a disagreement or they become unreachable, and you find you don't legally hold your own web address.

The person or business named as the **registrant** is the legal holder. That should be you.

So:

- Register it yourself, or make sure whoever does it registers it in your name.
- Make sure you have the login to the account where it's registered.
- Turn on auto-renew, and keep the payment card up to date.
- Use an email address for the account that you'll always have access to.

## What it costs

Domains are one of the cheaper parts of running a website, but prices vary between registrars, and introductory prices often jump on renewal. Always check the **renewal** price, not just the first-year offer.

## Email uses your domain too

Your domain isn't only for your website. It's also how you get an email address like hello@yourbusiness.co.uk, which looks far more established on a quote than a free webmail address.

That's another reason to keep the domain short. You'll be typing it, and so will your customers, every time they email you.

## A simple checklist

1. Short, easy to say and spell?
2. Based on your business name, without hyphens?
3. Ending chosen for your customers (.co.uk for most UK businesses)?
4. Obvious alternatives registered too, if affordable?
5. Registered in your name, in an account you can log in to?
6. Auto-renew turned on?

Get those six right and your domain will quietly do its job for years.
