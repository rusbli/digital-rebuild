One morning the calls stop. You search for your business and your listing has gone from the map. Or you log in and see a message saying your profile has been suspended.

It's alarming, especially if most of your work comes through Google. But a suspension isn't always permanent, and the way you respond in the next few days matters.

Here's what tends to cause it, what to do first, and how to appeal properly.

> **Quick answer:** Don't create a new profile. Check your profile against Google's guidelines, especially your business name, address and service area, and fix anything that doesn't comply. Then submit an appeal through Google's process with evidence that your business is real and eligible, such as registration documents or utility bills. Keep your evidence clear and consistent with your profile.

Three questions we'll answer:

- What usually triggers a suspension?
- What evidence actually helps an appeal?
- How do I stop it happening again?

## First, don't make it worse

The instinct is to create a new profile and start again. Don't. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) allow one profile per business location, and a duplicate can make the situation harder to resolve.

Also avoid making lots of changes in quick succession. Fix what needs fixing, once, then appeal.

## What usually triggers a suspension

Here's the first question. Google doesn't always tell you exactly why. But most suspensions trace back to the guidelines. Check these in order:

### The business name

Google's guidelines say your name should match the name you use in the real world: on your signs, van, invoices and website. Adding extra words, like "Plumber Cardiff 24/7", breaks the rules.

If your profile name includes anything beyond your real trading name, that's the most likely cause.

### The address

- **Virtual offices and mailboxes** aren't eligible.
- **If you visit customers** rather than them visiting you, Google says you should hide your address and set a service area.
- **If you share space**, such as a co-working office, there are extra conditions about signage and staffing.

### The service area

Google's guidelines say service areas generally shouldn't extend beyond about two hours' drive from your base. A service area covering the whole of the UK from a single home address can raise questions.

### Recent changes

A burst of edits (new name, new address, new category) can trigger a review. So can changes made by someone else with access to the profile.

### Categories and content

Categories should describe what your business **is**, not a list of services. Posts, photos and other content also have to follow Google's policies.

## Fix the problem before appealing

Once you've found the likely cause, fix it:

- Change the name to your real trading name.
- Hide your address and set a sensible service area, if you work at customers' locations.
- Remove anything that doesn't follow the guidelines.

Then make sure your website, invoices and other listings show the same name and details. Consistency is part of what makes a business look genuine.

## How to appeal

Google's help page on [fixing suspended or disabled profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145) explains the current process, and its page on [appealing restrictions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551) covers the appeals tool.

The appeal is where evidence matters.

## Evidence that helps

Here's the second question. Google is trying to confirm that your business is real, operates as described, and is entitled to the profile. Useful evidence includes:

- **Official registration**, such as Companies House details for a limited company.
- **A business licence or certification**, if your trade has one.
- **Utility bills or bank statements** in the business name, showing the address you've given Google.
- **Photos** of your signage, van or premises showing your business name.
- **Your website**, showing the same name, phone number and area.

Make sure everything matches. If your evidence shows a slightly different name or address from your profile, it undermines the appeal.

Google's appeals page also notes that once you open the evidence form, you have a limited time to submit it. So gather everything **before** you start.

## While you wait

- **Don't keep editing the profile.**
- **Make sure your website is doing its job.** People who still find you through ordinary search results need to be able to call. My guide to [showing up in near-me searches](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-near-me-searches/) explains how your website and profile work together.
- **Tell regular customers** how to reach you directly, if the suspension drags on.

If the first appeal is refused, Google's help pages describe how to ask for a further review. Be wary of anyone promising guaranteed reinstatement for a fee; Google makes the final decision.

## The aha: suspensions often start with "small" shortcuts

Many suspensions start with something that seemed harmless at the time: an extra keyword in the name, an address that wasn't quite right, a service area set as wide as possible.

They might even have worked for a while. That's what makes them tempting. But they put the whole profile at risk, and the profile is often worth far more than the small boost.

## How to stop it happening again

Here's the third question.

1. **Use your real name**, exactly as it appears on your signs and paperwork.
2. **Use an eligible address**, or hide it and set a realistic service area.
3. **Choose accurate categories.**
4. **Limit who has access** to the profile, and check who's listed as a manager.
5. **Make changes sparingly**, and only when something genuinely changes.
6. **Keep your details consistent** across your website and other listings.
